<h2 id="the-two-thresholds-that-determine-leaving-the-flat-rate-regime">The two thresholds that determine leaving the flat-rate regime</h2><p>In 2026 the flat-rate regime has two different thresholds with different effects. If during the year you exceed 85,000 euros but stay below 100,000 euros, you remain in the flat-rate regime for the whole current year and move to the ordinary or simplified regime only from the following year. If instead you exceed 100,000 euros, exit is immediate from the transaction that causes the excess: from that moment you must charge VAT on invoices and apply the ordinary rules in the same year, not from the following tax period.</p><h2 id="switching-without-prior-notice">Switching without prior notice</h2><p>Moving between regimes does not require prior notice to the Revenue Agency: so-called conclusive behaviour is enough, that is, starting to issue invoices under the rules of the new regime (with or without VAT as appropriate) from the moment the obligation arises or the option is exercised. This makes the switch operationally simple, but it requires care: an invoicing error at the time of the regime change, such as continuing to issue invoices without VAT when VAT now has to be applied, exposes you to penalties for incorrect invoicing.</p><h2 id="the-three-year-commitment-for-those-choosing-the-ordinary-regime">The three-year commitment for those choosing the ordinary regime</h2><p>Those moving from the flat-rate to the ordinary regime by choice, not because of a threshold obligation, are bound by that choice for at least three years: they cannot go back to the flat-rate regime before the commitment expires, even if in the meantime they would again meet all the requirements. The three-year commitment does not apply, however, to those leaving the flat-rate regime because they were forced to by exceeding the revenue thresholds: in that case a return is possible as soon as volumes fall back below the required threshold, with no forced waiting.</p><h2 id="2026-novelty-employment-income-threshold-at-35-000-euros">2026 novelty: employment income threshold at 35,000 euros</h2><p>The 2026 Budget Law extended the exemption allowing you to keep the flat-rate regime even with employment or pension income of up to 35,000 euros gross in the previous year, instead of the ordinary limit of 30,000 euros. It is a transitional measure: unless further extensions, from 2027 the threshold will return to the ordinary value of 30,000 euros. Those with a dual income from employment and a VAT number must check this threshold every year, because any failure to renew would trigger exclusion from the favourable regime even while staying below 85,000 euros of revenue.</p><h2 id="what-changes-concretely-in-management">What changes concretely in management</h2><p>Moving to the ordinary or simplified regime means having to apply VAT on all invoices issued, keeping complete accounting records (in the simplified regime: VAT registers, receipts and payments; in the ordinary: also the journal and inventories), and progressive IRPEF taxation on actual income instead of the fixed substitute tax. The flat-rate regime, by contrast, remains exempt from IRAP and continues to benefit from the 15% substitute tax (or 5% for new activities in the first five years), but with the limit that actual costs are not deductible, being replaced by the flat-rate profitability coefficient.</p><p><em>Changing regime has important practical and tax effects: always check your specific situation with an accountant before any decision or at the first sign of approaching the thresholds.</em></p>

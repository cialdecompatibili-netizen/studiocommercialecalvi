<p>Tax rules vary greatly depending on whether the counterparty is in another EU country or outside the European Union: confusing the two cases is one of the most common mistakes of those starting to work abroad.</p><h2 id="scambi-intra-ue">Intra-EU trade</h2><p>Supplies of goods to businesses in other EU countries are generally not subject to VAT in Italy (intra-Community transactions), provided you are registered in VIES and can prove the actual transport of the goods out of the national territory.</p><h2 id="esportazioni-extra-ue">Non-EU exports</h2><p>Supplies to non-EU countries are not subject to VAT if supported by customs proof of the goods leaving the EU customs territory: without this proof, the transaction risks being treated as taxable on audit.</p><h2 id="importazioni">Imports</h2><p>Import VAT applies at customs when the goods enter, except under suspension regimes or the reverse charge mechanism where provided: the customs code and origin of the goods must always be checked.</p><h2 id="esportatore-abituale-e-plafond-iva">Habitual exporter and VAT ceiling</h2><p>Anyone carrying out exports and intra-Community transactions worth more than 10% of their turnover acquires "habitual exporter" status and can buy or import goods and services without VAT, up to a ceiling calculated on the exports recorded in the previous calendar year (fixed ceiling) or in the previous 12 months (moving ceiling). To use it you must electronically submit a declaration of intent to the Revenue Agency before the transaction: since 2020 it no longer has to be handed to the supplier, but the protocol number must still be communicated because the supplier is required to check it and show it on the invoice.</p><div class="art-nota"><p>Before starting business relationships abroad, it is always worth checking in advance the correct VAT and customs treatment of the transaction, not after the fact when the goods have already left.</p></div>

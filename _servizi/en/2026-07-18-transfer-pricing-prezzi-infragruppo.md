<p>When a company sells goods or services to a group affiliate, at what price does it do so? The answer is not as obvious as it seems, and the subject that deals with it is called transfer pricing. It concerns every group with companies in several countries, even small ones, not just large multinationals.</p><h2 id="the-arm-s-length-principle">The arm's length principle</h2><p>The basic rule is simple to state: transactions between companies of the same group must take place on the same terms that would have been agreed between independent businesses on the free market. In Italy this principle is codified in article 110, paragraph 7 of the TUIR and follows article 9 of the OECD Model Convention. It exists to prevent groups from artificially shifting profits to the country with the lowest taxation, inflating or deflating intra-group prices at will.</p><p>A concrete example: a group with its parent in Italy (corporate income tax at 24%) and a subsidiary in a lower-tax country has, on paper, an incentive to keep the selling price to that subsidiary low, so that the margin emerges there. This is exactly the kind of transaction that transfer pricing rules aim to catch.</p><h2 id="which-transactions-it-covers">Which transactions it covers</h2><p>Transfer pricing applies to any type of intra-group transaction: sale of goods (raw materials, semi-finished and finished products), provision of services (administration, IT, marketing, management fees), licences and royalties on brands and know-how, intra-group loans, guarantees and cash pooling.</p><h2 id="the-methods-for-determining-the-correct-price">The methods for determining the correct price</h2><p>The OECD identifies several methods for establishing whether an intra-group price respects the arm's length principle. The comparable uncontrolled price (CUP) method compares the intra-group price with that of comparable transactions between independent parties: it works well for listed raw materials, less so for unique goods or services. The resale price method starts from the price at which the subsidiary resells to third parties and subtracts a market margin to arrive at the correct purchase price. The cost plus method, instead, starts from the costs of the producer or service provider and adds a margin consistent with that of an independent operator: it is the most used for contract manufacturing and intra-group services.</p><h2 id="what-those-who-do-not-comply-risk">What those who do not comply risk</h2><p>Tax authorities now exchange information far more easily than in the past, and checks on intra-group transactions have become more frequent even for SMEs with international activity, not just large groups. If an audit finds prices not aligned with the market, the risk is an assessment recovering the difference for taxation, penalties and interest — with the aggravating factor that the challenge often covers several tax years together.</p><h2 id="how-we-can-help">How we can help</h2><p>We help you map your group's intra-group transactions, choose the most suitable pricing method for each type of transaction and set up a consistent and sustainable transfer pricing policy, reducing the risk of challenges in an audit.</p>

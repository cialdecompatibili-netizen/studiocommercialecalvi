<p>IRAP and Form 770 are two separate obligations that often overlap in the same taxpayer's calendar: one taxes the net production value of companies and entities, the other certifies the tax withheld as a withholding agent. Confusing them — or forgetting that sole proprietors and freelancers have no longer paid IRAP since 2022 — is a common mistake.</p><h2 id="irap-who-pays-and-who-is-excluded">IRAP: who pays and who is excluded</h2><p>Since 2022, owners of sole proprietorships and freelancers working individually are excluded from IRAP. Still liable are: partnerships and companies, associated professional firms with an autonomous organisation, commercial and public entities, and non-residents with a permanent establishment in Italy. The ordinary rate is 3.9% of net production value, with Regions able to vary it by up to ±0.92 points or set it to zero in some cases.</p><h2 id="irap-deadlines-2026">IRAP deadlines 2026</h2><ul><li><strong>30 June</strong> — balance for the previous year and first instalment (normally 40% of the tax due), or 30 July with a 0.40% surcharge</li><li><strong>30 September</strong> — electronic submission of the IRAP return via Entratel/Fisconline</li><li><strong>30 November</strong> — second instalment (the remaining 60%, or the whole instalment in a single payment if not paid in June)</li></ul><p>The first instalment is due only if it exceeds 103 €; below this threshold everything is paid in November.</p><h2 id="form-770-who-is-required">Form 770: who is required</h2><p>Form 770 certifies the tax withheld by the withholding agent during the year (on employment, self-employment, dividends, interest on securities and bonds). Also required to file are bankruptcy trustees, liquidation commissioners and heirs who continue the activity of a deceased withholding agent, as well as anyone who applied substitute tax on interest/dividends in the reference year.</p><h2 id="why-keep-them-separate-in-planning">Why keep them separate in planning</h2><p>IRAP and 770 have different populations of obliged taxpayers and deadlines that do not always coincide: a company may be required to file both, while a freelancer with no employees or collaborators may need to file neither. Correctly checking your own scope of obligation avoids both penalised omissions and unnecessary filings.</p><h2 id="how-we-manage-irap-and-770-for-our-clients">How we manage IRAP and 770 for our clients</h2><p>Every year we check whether the company or professional falls within the IRAP scope (it often changes with legal form or the organisation of the business), calculate instalments and balance on the estimated or historical production value, and handle data collection for Form 770 throughout the year — not just close to the deadline — to avoid errors in the final September rush.</p>

<p>Predicting whether the company will have enough cash at quarter end, or which customers risk paying late, no longer requires a structured finance department: artificial intelligence applied to predictive analysis makes these tools accessible to SMEs too, overcoming the limits of traditional spreadsheets based only on historical data.</p><h2 id="what-predictive-analysis-actually-does">What predictive analysis actually does</h2><p>Unlike simple historical reporting — a snapshot of what has already happened — predictive analysis builds a film that follows the company month after month, integrating historical data, customer payment patterns, seasonality and external variables to generate realistic future scenarios. The most concrete applications for an SME include:</p><ul><li><strong>Cash flow forecasting</strong> — estimating future cash flows based on historical invoicing and customer payment behaviour, often over 12-36 month horizons</li><li><strong>Anomaly detection</strong> — automatic identification of duplicate invoices, unusual amounts or abnormal variations from the usual pattern</li><li><strong>Collections forecasting</strong> — estimating when and whether a receivable will actually be collected, based on each customer's history</li></ul><h2 id="how-much-data-you-really-need">How much data you really need</h2><p>You don't need millions of records: with 12-24 months of structured historical data (invoicing, receipts, payments) it is already possible to build useful predictive models. Data quality matters more than quantity — incomplete or poorly structured data produces unreliable forecasts, however sophisticated the algorithm.</p><h2 id="the-advantage-of-a-timely-signal">The advantage of a timely signal</h2><p>The real value is not the forecast itself, but the chance to catch a gap between forecast and actual results the moment it appears — while there is still room to act: renegotiating a payment, chasing a late receipt, arranging a credit line in time. Waiting for the year-end financial statements to notice a liquidity problem is often too late to fix it.</p><h2 id="beware-the-limits-of-historical-data">Beware the limits of historical data</h2><p>Predictive models learn from the data they receive: if the history contains distortions (a customer who stopped paying, an exceptional one-off event), the model can replicate or amplify that distortion in future forecasts. Human oversight of the results remains essential, not an unnecessary step.</p><h2 id="how-we-integrate-predictive-analysis-into-our-work-with-clients">How we integrate predictive analysis into our work with clients</h2><p>We build forecast cash flow dashboards updated periodically, not just at year end, monitor gaps between forecast and actual results to step in promptly, and use invoicing history to anticipate the risk of late collections — a safeguard that, managed in outsourcing with our firm, costs far less than building a dedicated internal structure.</p>

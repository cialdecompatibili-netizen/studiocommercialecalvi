<p>Anyone opening a VAT number must enrol in an INPS pension fund, but it is not always the same one: it depends on the type of activity carried out. The main distinction is between the Separate Fund (Gestione Separata), reserved for professionals without their own pension fund, and the Artisans and Traders funds, which follow very different calculation rules. A mistake in classification means paying contributions into the wrong fund, with anomalies that often emerge only after years.</p><h2 id="separate-fund-who-must-enrol">Separate Fund: who must enrol</h2><p>The INPS Separate Fund, established by Law 335/1995, is the pension fund for self-employed workers without a professional fund of their own: professionals with a VAT number not registered with an order with its own fund (developers, consultants, designers, trainers), coordinated and continuous collaborators, company directors and auditors, research fellows and PhD students. Those enrolled in a professional fund (lawyers with Cassa Forense, accountants, doctors, etc.) follow the rules of their own fund, not those of the Separate Fund — even if they occasionally carry out another professional activity.</p><h2 id="how-contributions-are-calculated-in-the-separate-fund">How contributions are calculated in the Separate Fund</h2><p>The key difference from artisans and traders is that in the Separate Fund there is no fixed minimum contribution payable regardless of income: you pay only a percentage of what you actually earn, up to an annual ceiling updated each year by INPS. For 2026 (INPS circular no. 8/2026) the rate for professionals with a VAT number not enrolled in another pension fund is 26.07% of taxable income; it drops to 24% for those already retired or enrolled in another compulsory fund; it rises to 35.03% for collaborators without a VAT number entitled to DIS-COLL. On income above the ceiling (122,295 euros for 2026) no further contributions are paid: those earning above this threshold still pay the maximum, not a proportionally higher amount.</p><h2 id="artisans-and-traders-the-opposite-logic">Artisans and Traders: the opposite logic</h2><p>Those carrying out craft or commercial activity follow instead the Artisans and Traders funds, structured differently: here there is a fixed minimum contribution due even at zero income, plus a variable share on the part of income above a given threshold. It is the opposite of the Separate Fund, where "if you don't earn, you don't pay". Flat-rate taxpayers enrolled in these funds (not in the Separate Fund) can also request a 35% reduction on the contributions due.</p><h2 id="why-the-distinction-matters">Why the distinction matters</h2><p>Correct enrolment depends on the prevailing activity carried out: manual or commercial for the artisan/trader funds, intellectual without a fund for the Separate Fund. An initial classification error is not just a formal problem: it can lead to paying wrong amounts for years, with consequences for the calculation of benefits, penalties for non-payment and difficulty regularising the position retroactively.</p><h2 id="how-we-can-help">How we can help</h2><p>We help you check from the moment you open your VAT number which INPS fund applies to your activity, correctly calculate the contributions due under the rates in force and plan payment deadlines, avoiding classification errors that are only discovered years later.</p>

<p>The research and development tax credit is one of the most concrete tools for those investing in innovation: no call is needed, and there is no ranking to win. It accrues automatically when you incur the right expenses, and is used directly to pay less tax.</p><h2 id="quanto-vale-nel-2026">How much it is worth in 2026</h2><p>For 2026 the base rate for research and development is 10% of eligible expenses, with a maximum limit of 5 million euros a year. For small businesses operating in Southern Italy (Abruzzo, Basilicata, Calabria, Campania, Molise, Puglia, Sardinia and Sicily) there are uplifts that can bring the benefit up to 45%: an important gap to know about if the business has its head office or production unit in those regions.</p><h2 id="cosa-rientra-nelle-spese-ammissibili">What counts as eligible expenses</h2><p>Staff employed in research activities, materials and supplies for prototypes, research contracts commissioned from universities or research centres, depreciation of tools and equipment used in the projects. A technical report is needed that documents precisely the link between the expense and the research activity.</p><h2 id="come-si-utilizza">How it is used</h2><p>The credit is used by offsetting on form F24, in three equal annual instalments, starting from the tax period following the one in which the expenses were incurred. It does not count towards taxable income for personal/corporate income tax (IRPEF/IRES) or the IRAP tax base.</p><div class="art-nota"><p>The technical documentation must be prepared carefully from the start: checks can arrive up to 10 years later, with heavy penalties if the report does not hold up on audit.</p></div>

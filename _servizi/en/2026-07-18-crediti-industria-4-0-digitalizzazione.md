<h2 id="dal-2026-cambia-tutto-addio-credito-d-imposta-arriva-l-iperammortamento">From 2026 everything changes: goodbye tax credit, hello super-depreciation</h2><p>From 1 January 2026 the Transition 4.0 tax credit no longer exists. In its place the 2026-2028 Super-depreciation (Law 199/2025) has come into force, radically changing the incentive mechanism: no longer a credit to offset on form F24, but an increase in the tax-deductible cost of the asset purchased. Those who invested by 31 December 2025, or paid a deposit of at least 20% by that date, remain under the transitional regime of the old tax credit; those investing from 2026 onwards fall under the new system.</p><h2 id="come-funziona-la-maggiorazione-del-costo">How the cost uplift works</h2><p>The mechanism is simple in principle, different in substance from the old credit: the asset still costs what was paid, but for tax purposes the company can deduct an increased base, up to 180% of the cost for tangible and intangible 4.0 assets with the required technical characteristics (control by CNC/PLC, interconnection, integration into company systems). In practice, a machine bought for 1 million euros can generate a tax-depreciable base of 2.8 million euros. The benefit does not produce immediate liquidity like an offsettable credit, but is spread over time through lower taxes paid in subsequent years, following the asset's depreciation schedule.</p><h2 id="le-aliquote-50-100-180">The rates: 50%, 100%, 180%</h2><p>The uplift varies according to the type of investment: from 50% to 180% depending on the asset category and group (tangible/intangible 4.0 capital goods under Annexes A and B to Law 232/2016, or assets for producing energy from renewable sources for self-consumption, including storage systems). Compared with the rates of the old 2025 4.0 credit (which stopped at 20-35%), the new nominal values are often more generous for companies with good tax capacity, even though the benefit is spread over time instead of being immediately offsettable.</p><h2 id="nessun-click-day-ma-tempistiche-da-rispettare">No click day, but timelines to respect</h2><p>Unlike the old Transition 5.0 Plan, which was quickly exhausted when funds ran out with many applications rejected or suspended, super-depreciation is a structural tool, not tied to a first-come funding ceiling: it therefore reduces the risk of click days and early closures. A precise time window remains, however: investments made by 31 December 2026 are covered, with a tail until 30 June 2027 for deliveries of assets for which a deposit of at least 20% was paid by the end of 2026.</p><h2 id="cosa-succede-se-il-bene-viene-sostituito-o-ceduto">What happens if the asset is replaced or sold</h2><p>If during the period in which the uplift is used the asset is sold or moved to production facilities abroad, the remaining benefit does not lapse automatically: provided that in the same tax period the company replaces the asset with a new capital good of similar or higher technological characteristics, the remaining instalments of the benefit continue as originally calculated. If the cost of the new investment is lower than that of the asset replaced, the benefit still continues up to the amount of the new cost.</p><p><em>A field subject to rapid regulatory change: always check the specific technical requirements of your investment and the most convenient depreciation strategy with an accountant.</em></p>

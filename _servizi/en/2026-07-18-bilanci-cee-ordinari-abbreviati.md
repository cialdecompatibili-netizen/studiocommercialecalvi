<p>Micro, abbreviated, ordinary: it is not a free choice, but the automatic result of exceeding (or not) precise thresholds for assets, revenue and employees over two consecutive years. Getting the form of financial statements wrong is not just a formal problem: it changes what you are required to put in writing and what you can legally leave out.</p><h2 id="i-tre-parametri-che-decidono-la-forma">The three parameters that decide the form</h2><p>The law always looks at the same three figures: total assets on the balance sheet, sales and services revenue, and the average number of employees during the year. To change the form of financial statements you must exceed (or fall back below) at least two of these three parameters, for two consecutive years — a single year above or below a threshold is not enough to trigger the change.</p><h2 id="le-soglie-aggiornate-d-lgs-125-2024">The updated thresholds (Legislative Decree 125/2024)</h2><p>From the 2024 financial statements onwards, the thresholds were raised by about 25% compared with the previous values:</p><ul><li><strong>Micro-enterprise</strong> — assets up to €220,000, revenue up to €440,000, a maximum of 5 average employees</li><li><strong>Abbreviated financial statements</strong> — assets up to €5,500,000, revenue up to €11,000,000, a maximum of 50 average employees</li><li><strong>Ordinary financial statements</strong> — compulsory above these thresholds, or for companies with securities listed on regulated markets regardless of size</li></ul><h2 id="cosa-cambia-in-concreto-nei-tre-formati">What actually changes across the three formats</h2><p>A micro-enterprise is exempt from the explanatory notes and the directors' report, provided it shows certain minimum mandatory information at the foot of the balance sheet (guarantees, commitments, contingent liabilities, fees to directors and auditors). Abbreviated financial statements allow several income statement and balance sheet items to be aggregated and exempt from the cash flow statement, but still require the explanatory notes in summary form. Ordinary financial statements are the full version: balance sheet, income statement, cash flow statement and extended explanatory notes, without simplifications.</p><h2 id="il-calcolo-dei-dipendenti-non-banale">Calculating employees is not trivial</h2><p>The average number of employees is not counted in whole steps — a part-timer weighs in proportion to hours worked, and anyone joining or leaving mid-year must be weighted on the actual days of presence. It is one of the most common mistakes: a company that looks below the threshold when you glance at current headcount may actually exceed it if the weighted average calculation is done correctly.</p><h2 id="perch-farsi-seguire-nella-scelta-della-forma">Why get help choosing the form</h2><p>Getting the form of financial statements wrong is not just a penalty risk: statements that are too simplified relative to the real parameters are worthless as information and can be challenged by shareholders, banks or the Revenue Agency on audit. Every year we check the company's position against the thresholds, monitor the two-year trend (not just the last year) and prepare the transition to the higher form before it becomes an obligation discovered at the last minute.</p>

<p>The Certificazione Unica is the document by which the withholding agent certifies the fees paid and the tax withheld during the previous year to employees, pensioners and professionals. From 2026 the deadlines are no longer the same for everyone: the main novelty is the split of deadlines according to the type of income.</p><h2 id="the-three-deadlines-of-2026">The three deadlines of 2026</h2><ul><li><strong>16 March</strong> — ordinary deadline for employment income, similar income, occasional self-employment and miscellaneous income. The summary copy must also be delivered to the recipient by the same date.</li><li><strong>30 April (new for 2026)</strong> — new deadline, postponed from the previous 31 March, for certifications on habitual self-employment income (professionals with a VAT number) and for commissions from agency, brokerage, sales representation and business-finding relationships.</li><li><strong>31 October (2 November in 2026, since the 31st falls on a Saturday)</strong> — residual deadline for certifications with exempt income or income that cannot otherwise be declared through the pre-filled return.</li></ul><h2 id="why-the-deadline-for-the-self-employed-changed">Why the deadline for the self-employed changed</h2><p>The postponement from 31 March to 30 April (Legislative Decree 81/2025) was introduced to give withholding agents more time to handle professionals' data, but it has a knock-on effect: the pre-filled return for VAT-number holders is consequently moved to 20 May, a few weeks after Form 730 (which remains set at 30 April).</p><h2 id="what-to-do-in-practice">What to do in practice</h2><p>Submitting the employment and self-employment sections separately, rather than in a single block, simplifies management when different incomes have different deadlines — and reduces the risk of being late on one section while waiting for all the data of the other to be ready.</p><h2 id="penalties-for-missing-or-late-submission">Penalties for missing or late submission</h2><p>The ordinary penalty for omitted, late or incorrect transmission is 100 euros per certification, with a cap of 50,000 euros per withholding agent per year. A repeated error across several certifications can therefore generate a significant penalty even for a single year.</p><h2 id="how-we-manage-the-cu-for-our-clients">How we manage the CU for our clients</h2><p>We monitor the two deadlines (16 March and 30 April) separately according to the type of income certified, prepare the certifications well in advance to avoid last-minute submissions, and check the consistency of the data with the tax withheld during the year before the final transmission to the Revenue Agency.</p>

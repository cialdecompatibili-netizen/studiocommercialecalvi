<p>The purchase VAT register, the sales VAT register and the depreciable asset register are the three accounting books that, together with periodic settlements, show the Revenue Agency that the company's accounts are traceable and consistent. With the RT-POS link obligation that came into force in 2026 the scope has widened further.</p><h2 id="purchase-and-sales-vat-registers">Purchase and sales VAT registers</h2><p>They track every invoice issued and received, with taxable amount, tax and rate applied. They are the basis from which periodic VAT settlements (monthly or quarterly) and the annual return are calculated. With mandatory e-invoicing, much of the completion is now automated directly from the SdI flow, but responsibility for checking the correctness of the data remains with the company.</p><h2 id="receipts-what-changed-in-2026">Receipts: what changed in 2026</h2><p>The old paper register of receipts has not been mandatory for years for those using a telematic cash register: daily storage and transmission to the Revenue Agency replaced it. The 2026 novelty concerns the integration between RT and electronic payment tools: from 1 January the obligation of a logical link between telematic cash register and POS (physical, virtual, wallet, payment apps) took effect, with aggregated daily transmission of payment data together with receipts. Those who issue only e-invoices and do not use an RT remain excluded from this specific obligation.</p><h2 id="depreciable-asset-register">Depreciable asset register</h2><p>It keeps track of each of the company's capital assets (machinery, equipment, furniture, vehicles): historical cost, annual depreciation charge, accumulated depreciation fund, any capital gain or loss on disposal. It is the tool that makes the calculation of charges deducted in the accounts and in the return defensible in an audit — depreciation challenged for lack of documentation on the asset generates a tax adjustment across several years at once, not just the year of the audit.</p><h2 id="why-consistency-between-registers-matters-more-than-any-single-deadline">Why consistency between registers matters more than any single deadline</h2><p>The real risk is not forgetting a single entry, but having registers that do not reconcile with each other: VAT payable from the periodic settlement different from that shown by the purchase/sales registers, or assets in the accounts without the register detail that justifies them. It is the first cross-check the Revenue Agency makes in an audit, even before looking at individual transactions.</p><h2 id="how-we-handle-record-keeping">How we handle record-keeping</h2><p>We keep all mandatory registers integrated with the accounting software, check consistency between receipts/invoices and periodic settlements before submission, and keep the asset register updated with every purchase, disposal or sale during the year — so that in case of an audit the documentation is already ready, not to be reconstructed afterwards.</p>

<h2 id="immediate-invoice-and-deferred-invoice-the-difference">Immediate invoice and deferred invoice: the difference</h2><p>The general rule requires the invoice to be issued at the time of the transaction, but the law also allows the deferred invoice for supplies of goods accompanied by a transport document (DDT) or equivalent document: in this case the invoice can be issued by the 15th day of the month following delivery, also summarising several transactions with the same customer in the same month. The document type code to use for the deferred invoice is TD24, precisely because it presupposes that a DDT or equivalent document has already been issued to evidence delivery.</p><h2 id="intra-eu-purchases-the-reverse-charge-mechanism">Intra-EU purchases: the reverse charge mechanism</h2><p>When you buy goods or services from a supplier established in another EU State, the supplier issues an invoice without charging Italian VAT: the Italian buyer must supplement the invoice received, applying the VAT rate provided by Italian law and recording the transaction both in the purchase register and in the sales register, with a substantially neutral effect on the VAT settlement. This mechanism is called reverse charge. The document code to send to the SdI is TD17 for the purchase of services from abroad and TD18 for the purchase of intra-Community goods.</p><h2 id="integration-deadlines-to-respect">Integration deadlines to respect</h2><p>The integration of the foreign invoice must be sent to the SdI by the 15th day of the month following receipt of the supplier's invoice or the date of the transaction. Failing to meet this deadline exposes you to penalties: for transactions from 2022 onwards, omitted integration carries a penalty of 2 euros per invoice, with a maximum of 400 euros per month, reducible to 200 euros if the transmission takes place within 15 days of the original deadline.</p><h2 id="what-happens-if-the-supplier-does-not-send-the-invoice">What happens if the supplier does not send the invoice</h2><p>If an EU supplier does not send the invoice, or sends it irregularly, the Italian buyer must still report the transaction to the Revenue Agency. From 1 April 2025 this report is made with code TD29 (which replaced the previous TD20), to be sent within 90 days of the transaction: failing to do so exposes you to a penalty of 70% of the VAT due. It is an obligation often overlooked precisely because it concerns a "passive" case (someone else's default), but responsibility for the report still falls on the buyer.</p><h2 id="non-eu-goods-the-difference-with-vat-at-customs">Non-EU goods: the difference with VAT at customs</h2><p>For purchases of goods from non-EU suppliers the logic changes: VAT is normally settled at customs on import, not through invoice integration. Code TD19 is used only in specific cases, applying when the foreign supplier (even non-EU) has an active VAT position in Italy or when the goods do not physically pass through Italian customs with the standard customs formalities. Correctly distinguishing between the two scenarios avoids applying the wrong TD code, which generates rejections or VAT settlement errors.</p><p><em>The rules on reverse charge and foreign invoices include many specific cases: always check with an accountant the correct document code for your transaction.</em></p>

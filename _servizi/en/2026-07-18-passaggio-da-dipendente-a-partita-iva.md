<p>Moving from an employment contract to self-employment radically changes how you pay tax, pay contributions and manage your cash over the year.</p><h2 id="from-payslip-to-tax-instalments">From payslip to tax instalments</h2><p>As an employee, tax is withheld monthly by the employer. With a VAT number, payment is made through an annual balance and instalments: this means setting aside the sums due yourself, instead of seeing them already deducted.</p><h2 id="social-security-coverage-changes-fund">Social security coverage changes fund</h2><p>You move from INPS contributions as an employee to a specific fund for the self-employed (separate fund, artisans, traders or professional fund), with different rates and payment methods.</p><h2 id="the-35-000-euro-limit-from-employment">The 35,000 euro limit from employment</h2><p>Those who received employment or similar income (including pensions) above 35,000 euros in the previous year cannot access the flat-rate regime. The limit does not apply, however, if the employment relationship ended by 31 December of the previous year and no new one is still running: this is precisely the typical situation of someone leaving a job to go it alone.</p><h2 id="beware-of-the-link-with-the-former-employer">Beware of the link with the former employer</h2><p>If in the first two years of self-employment revenue from the former employer (or parties linked to them) exceeds 50% of the total, a disqualifying condition arises that excludes you from the flat-rate regime. The rule does not apply if the employment relationship ended more than two tax periods before opening the VAT number, and some exceptions were introduced by Law 203/2024.</p><div class="art-nota"><p>Before leaving a job to open a VAT number, it is worth simulating the new tax and contribution burden, so as not to be short of cash at the first tax instalment.</p></div>

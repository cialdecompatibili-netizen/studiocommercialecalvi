<p>Anyone opening a VAT number as a craftsperson, trader or member of the Separate Scheme has to deal with two thresholds that INPS updates every year: the minimum, below which a fixed contribution is paid anyway, and the maximum, above which nothing more is paid. Understanding where your income sits relative to these two thresholds is essential to correctly calculate how much you must pay.</p><h2 id="il-minimale-si-paga-sempre-anche-a-reddito-zero">The minimum: always paid, even with zero income</h2><p>For craftspeople and traders, the 2026 minimum income is 18,808 euros. Those with business income equal to or below this figure still pay the fixed contribution calculated on this base: about 4,200 euros a year for craftspeople and slightly more for traders, because of the additional 0.48% for the business cessation indemnity fund. This contribution is due regardless of actual turnover, except for specific exemptions (for example a 50% reduction for those over 65 who are already pensioners, or for those registering for the first time).</p><h2 id="aliquote-2026">2026 rates</h2><ul><li><strong>Craftspeople:</strong> 24% on income above the minimum</li><li><strong>Traders:</strong> 24.48% (24% + an additional 0.48% for the indemnity fund)</li><li><strong>Separate Scheme:</strong> 26.07% (unchanged from 2025)</li></ul><h2 id="il-massimale-oltre-questa-soglia-non-si-versa-più">The maximum: above this threshold nothing more is paid</h2><p>Once a certain income is exceeded, contributions stop. For the Separate Scheme the 2026 maximum is 122,295 euros: above this figure, the contribution base stops there, regardless of how much more you earn. For craftspeople and traders the logic is different: those above the minimum pay fixed contributions + 24% on the excess up to a ceiling (89,461 euros in 2026), beyond which no further contributions apply.</p><h2 id="il-caso-del-regime-forfettario">The case of the flat-rate regime</h2><p>Those under the flat-rate regime who are registered with the craftspeople's/traders' schemes can ask INPS for a 35% reduction in the contributions due. The calculation is made on the flat-rate taxable income (applying the profitability coefficient of your ATECO code), not on gross turnover: if the contribution calculated on income is lower than the minimum, the minimum is paid anyway.</p><h2 id="un-esempio-pratico">A practical example</h2><p>A flat-rate craftsperson with 40,000 euros of turnover and a profitability coefficient of 67% has taxable income of 26,800 euros. Applying 24.48% (the traders' rate) gives a contribution of about 6,560 euros, above the minimum: in this case the calculated amount is paid, not the fixed minimum.</p><h2 id="dove-trovare-gli-importi-aggiornati">Where to find the updated amounts</h2><p>The exact amounts to pay for the current year can always be found in the INPS Social Security Drawer, in the "F24 form data" section: it is the most reliable source because it reflects the ISTAT updates applied each year, without having to recalculate by hand every time.</p><p>Getting the calculation wrong between minimum, maximum and the right rate is one of the most common mistakes among those who open a VAT number on their own: an incorrect calculation easily leads to insufficient payments, with interest and penalties to be recovered later.</p>

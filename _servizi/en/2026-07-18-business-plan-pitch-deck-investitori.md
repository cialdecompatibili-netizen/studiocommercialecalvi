<p>A business plan and a pitch deck are not the same document, and confusing them is one of the most common mistakes of first-time startups. They serve different moments on the path to the investor, and presenting them the wrong way round can cost you credibility before you even talk about numbers.</p><h2 id="business-plan-il-documento-che-regge-il-round">Business plan: the document that backs the round</h2><p>A modern startup business plan follows an essential 9-section structure, recognised by investors, banks and accelerators: executive summary, problem, solution, market, business model, go-to-market strategy, team, financial plan, investment request. It is not bureaucracy: the very process of writing it exposes flaws in the model that must be fixed before seeking serious capital.</p><p>There are two versions useful at different times:</p><ul><li><strong>Lean business plan</strong> (5-10 pages): quick to write and update, suited to seed rounds and to internal use to guide decisions</li><li><strong>Traditional business plan</strong> (complete): needed for substantial rounds, bank financing and public calls such as Smart&amp;Start, where completeness reduces the risk perceived by the assessors</li></ul><h2 id="pitch-deck-lo-strumento-per-il-primo-incontro">Pitch deck: the tool for the first meeting</h2><p>The pitch deck is a short presentation — typically 10-12 slides — designed to be quick and punchy, not to replace the business plan. A widely cited reference is Guy Kawasaki's 10/20/30 rule: 10 slides, 20 minutes of presentation, minimum font size 30 points. It serves to select only the truly relevant information and leave room for questions, not to tell everything.</p><p>Investors spend on average less than four minutes on the first review of a deck: it must be clear and readable at a glance, not a business plan squeezed into slides.</p><p>Typical structure: opening with name and tagline, problem, solution, market size, business model, traction (real numbers, not projections), team, investment request and how the capital will be used.</p><h2 id="l-obiettivo-reale-del-pitch">The real goal of the pitch</h2><p>An effective pitch deck rarely closes an investment at the first meeting: its purpose is to generate enough interest to earn a second meeting, where you then go into detail with the full business plan. The two documents work in sequence, not as alternatives.</p><h2 id="come-ti-aiutiamo">How we help you</h2><p>We build the business plan with realistic, consistent financial projections (the first reason for rejection in public calls is vague numbers not backed by credible assumptions), and we help you condense the pitch deck so that the two documents tell the same story at the right depth for each audience.</p>

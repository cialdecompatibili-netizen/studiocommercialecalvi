<h2 id="ogni-piattaforma-ha-regole-fiscali-diverse">Each platform has different tax rules</h2><p>Amazon FBA, Shopify and dropshipping look like variants of the same business, but from a tax standpoint they are not at all equivalent. With Amazon FBA, warehousing and shipping are handled by Amazon, but responsibility for invoicing, bookkeeping and declaring revenue always stays with the seller. With Shopify you run your own e-commerce: no intermediary platform takes care of VAT or invoicing, everything falls on the owner. In dropshipping the seller does not own the goods: they collect from the end customer and pass the order to a third-party supplier, often outside the EU, who ships directly.</p><h2 id="partita-iva-sempre-obbligatoria">VAT number: always required</h2><p>Selling online on a regular basis always amounts to a business activity, even part-time or alongside an employed job. Since 2023, platforms such as Amazon and Etsy have reported seller data to the Revenue Agency, so operating without a VAT number exposes you to a real risk of assessment.</p><h2 id="regime-forfettario-il-punto-critico-sulle-commissioni">Flat-rate regime: the critical point on fees</h2><p>Most new sellers start under the flat-rate regime: a 15% substitute tax (5% in the first 5 years if the requirements are met) on a profitability coefficient of 40% for trade, no VAT on invoices, simplified bookkeeping. Beware of a common mistake, though: the revenue that counts for the 85,000-euro limit is the gross amount of the sale, not the net after the fees withheld by Amazon or the platform. If you sell for 100 euros and the platform withholds 15 euros in fees, your tax revenue stays at 100 euros: anyone approaching the ceiling must calculate it on this basis, not on what they actually receive.</p><h2 id="perché-il-forfettario-spesso-non-conviene-nel-dropshipping">Why the flat-rate regime often does not pay off in dropshipping</h2><p>In dropshipping with EU or non-EU suppliers, the flat-rate regime has a structural limit: it does not allow you to deduct VAT on purchases from suppliers. Those working with tight margins and frequent purchases from foreign suppliers must therefore assess case by case whether the ordinary or simplified regime is more convenient, despite the favourable taxation on paper.</p><h2 id="contributi-inps-e-vendite-a-distanza">INPS contributions and distance sales</h2><p>Those who sell physical goods pay INPS contributions to the Traders' Scheme; under the flat-rate regime you can request a 35% reduction. For sales to private individuals in other EU countries above the threshold of 10,000 euros a year, the OSS scheme comes into play: it avoids VAT registration in every single destination country but is not an exemption, as the VAT of the customer's country is still due.</p><p><em>The information is up to date with 2026 rules and is provided for general guidance: always check your specific situation with an accountant before choosing your tax regime.</em></p>

<h2 id="when-it-makes-sense-to-set-up-a-holding-company">When it makes sense to set up a holding company</h2><p>A holding is a company whose main activity is holding shareholdings in other companies, not directly producing goods or services. It is worth considering when there are several operating companies to coordinate, when you need to separate personal assets from business risks, or when the entrepreneur wants to plan generational transfer without having to hand shares directly to heirs. It is not a tool for everyone: setting up and maintaining a holding involves running costs and obligations that must be justified by a real strategic benefit, not just a tax one.</p><h2 id="the-tax-advantage-on-intra-group-dividends">The tax advantage on intra-group dividends</h2><p>One of the most concrete reasons to set up a holding is the favourable tax regime on dividends that operating companies distribute to the holding itself: a significant share of the distributed profit may be exempt from tax at holding level, deferring full taxation to when the profit is distributed to individual shareholders. This allows capital to be reinvested between group companies with a very low intermediate tax burden, instead of taxing every step as if it were personal income.</p><h2 id="asset-protection-not-just-tax-planning">Asset protection, not just tax planning</h2><p>A well-structured holding isolates the assets held (shareholdings, property, cash) from the operating risks of the individual subsidiaries: if an operating company runs into difficulty, the assets held by the holding remain separate, except in cases of specific liability provided by law. This separation is one of the reasons holdings are also used by those with no particular tax needs, who simply want to secure the wealth built up over time.</p><h2 id="generational-transfer-a-tool-not-an-automatic-solution">Generational transfer: a tool, not an automatic solution</h2><p>For family businesses, the holding allows the transfer of shares to heirs to be structured gradually and in a controlled way, for example through family pacts or progressive allocations of shares with governance clauses that keep operational control with whoever runs the business, even before final transfer of ownership. It is not an automatic solution: it requires a project built on the specific family and corporate situation, with a time horizon of years, not a last-minute intervention.</p><h2 id="common-mistakes-in-setting-up">Common mistakes in setting up</h2><p>The most frequent risk is setting up a holding without real economic substance - a paper-only structure, lacking its own organisation and decision-making - which exposes it to challenge by the Revenue Agency for abuse of law or fictitious interposition. A holding must be run like a real company: corporate bodies that actually pass resolutions, its own accounting, a documentable group logic. Only then do the tax advantage and asset protection stand up to scrutiny.</p><p><em>Setting up a holding must always be assessed case by case based on the real corporate and family situation: consult an accountant before starting any structure.</em></p>

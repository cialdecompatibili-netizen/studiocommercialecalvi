<h2 id="why-manual-management-doesn-t-hold-up-beyond-a-certain-volume">Why manual management doesn't hold up beyond a certain volume</h2><p>Anyone selling across several channels - their own site, Amazon, eBay, various marketplaces - soon faces the same problem: every order must become a valid electronic invoice, within the legal deadlines, with no errors in the customer's data. The typical manual flow is fragmented: the order arrives from the platform, is exported or re-entered by hand into the management system, the customer is checked, the invoice is generated and sent to the SDI, and only at the end is the order status updated. With a few orders a day it works; as volumes grow, every manual step becomes a point of error and an administrative bottleneck.</p><h2 id="how-the-automated-integration-works">How the automated integration works</h2><p>An integration between the e-commerce platform, the management system and the Sistema di Interscambio removes the manual steps: the order confirmed on the platform automatically generates the electronic invoice in the required XML format, sends it to the SDI and updates the management system status with no human intervention. The SDI checks the formal correctness of the file and delivers it to the recipient; the invoice must be issued within 12 days of the transaction and the SDI has 5 days to process it, with a further 5 days to correct and resubmit in case of rejection. Automating these steps means meeting deadlines even when orders arrive in a rush, without having to monitor them one by one.</p><h2 id="multi-channel-means-data-to-reconcile-not-just-invoices-to-issue">Multi-channel means data to reconcile, not just invoices to issue</h2><p>The most delicate part of multi-channel invoicing is not generating the document, but keeping data aligned across different systems: PayPal or Stripe receipts that do not automatically match recorded revenue, different customer records across platforms, VAT rates that change depending on the destination country. A common mistake is precisely the lack of reconciliation between payment processor receipts and booked revenue: without automation, this check has to be done by hand order by order, with an error margin that grows with volume.</p><h2 id="what-it-takes-to-make-it-work-well">What it takes to make it work well</h2><p>A stable integration requires a management system able to generate, receive and archive electronic invoices handling the necessary format conversions, clean customer and supplier records (correct identification codes, otherwise invoices are automatically rejected), and compliant long-term digital storage for all tax documents. The goal is not just to issue invoices automatically, but to build a system that keeps working without corrective intervention even when orders increase and processes become more complex.</p><h2 id="the-evolving-european-framework-vida-and-peppol">The evolving European framework: ViDA and Peppol</h2><p>The European VAT reform ViDA introduces mandatory B2B e-invoicing for cross-border transactions, with near real-time reporting: companies will have to send invoices within a few days of the transaction, no longer on current timelines. The Italian SDI will remain the national channel, but for intra-EU exchanges it will be necessary to integrate the Peppol network. Those who automate multi-channel invoicing today are in a better position to adapt when these deadlines become operational.</p><p><em>The information is up to date as of 2026 and is for general guidance: always check the technical and tax set-up best suited to your business with an accountant.</em></p>

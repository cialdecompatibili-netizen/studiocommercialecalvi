<p>An employee who suffers an accident or falls ill triggers different obligations depending on the case: the competent body, the communication deadlines and the procedures all change. Knowing the differences avoids mistakes that can cost the employer penalties or cost the employee the loss of financial benefits.</p><h2 id="ordinary-sickness-the-role-of-inps">Ordinary sickness: the role of INPS</h2><p>In ordinary sickness, not linked to a work event, the medical certificate is sent electronically to INPS directly by the family doctor. The employee must still notify the employer immediately, normally before the start of the scheduled shift. During sickness, apart from exceptions in the CCNL, the worker must be available at home during set time bands (typically 10:00-12:00 and 17:00-19:00 in the private sector) for possible check visits.</p><h2 id="workplace-accident-the-role-of-inail-and-the-deadlines-to-meet">Workplace accident: the role of INAIL and the deadlines to meet</h2><p>An accident follows a different procedure, handled by INAIL. The worker is required, under art. 52 of Presidential Decree 1124/65, to report the accident immediately to the employer, manager or supervisor — even for minor injuries. Late notification has a direct and heavy consequence: the worker loses the right to the financial benefit for all the days before the one on which the company formally became aware of the event. The first medical certificate, issued by the emergency room or a certifying doctor, must be sent to INAIL within 48 hours (24 hours in case of a fatal event) through the employer. The formal report to INAIL is mandatory only if the prognosis exceeds 3 days; below this threshold it is an accident without compensation. Unlike ordinary sickness, an injured worker is not required to observe the availability time bands for check visits.</p><h2 id="2026-changes-in-certificate-management">2026 changes in certificate management</h2><p>With INAIL circulars no. 17 and no. 23 of 2026, the Institute updated the procedures for closing cases without a final medical certificate, distinguishing three scenarios (including return to work without a final certificate at the end of the stated prognosis) and simplified the 1SS form used by doctors, reducing the data to be entered (it is no longer necessary to state the date the worker left the workplace or prior injuries). Since 13 May 2026 a new online service on the INAIL portal has been active for completing and sending certificates, designed to speed up the exchange of information between doctor, worker and company.</p><h2 id="what-the-employer-must-do-in-practice">What the employer must do in practice</h2><p>On the management side, the company must record the absence correctly with the right reason code, always distinguishing sickness from accident: a wrong code causes cascading errors in the calculation of accruals and deferred benefits. For accidents, the company generally advances the benefit in the payslip as provided by the applicable CCNL, and is then reimbursed by INAIL. Organisational cover must also be managed: an accident creates an immediate operational gap, which HR and department heads must fill by redistributing shifts.</p><h2 id="how-we-can-help">How we can help</h2><p>We help you set up clear internal procedures for handling sickness and accidents, checking communication deadlines, correct reason codes in the payslip and the correct adjustment with INPS and INAIL, to avoid errors that weigh on both the company and the employee.</p>

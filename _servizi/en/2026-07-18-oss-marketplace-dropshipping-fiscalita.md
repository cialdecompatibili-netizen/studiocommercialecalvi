<p>Anyone selling on Amazon, eBay or Etsy soon runs into something that owners of physical shops do not know: sometimes the platform itself has to pay the VAT in your place, and it is not always easy to tell when this happens and when it does not.</p><h2 id="marketplaces-who-really-pays-the-vat">Marketplaces: who really pays the VAT</h2><p>On platforms such as Amazon or eBay, for certain categories of sales (in particular non-EU sales below a certain value threshold) it is the marketplace itself that must apply and pay the VAT, acting as the 'deemed supplier'. This does not, however, relieve the seller of invoicing obligations in the other cases, which remain the majority of sales.</p><h2 id="dropshipping-the-triangulations-to-document">Dropshipping: the triangulations to document</h2><p>In dropshipping the goods often never physically pass through the seller: they are shipped directly from the supplier, often non-EU, to the end customer. This creates a triangulation that must be carefully documented, because the VAT treatment changes significantly depending on whether supplier, seller and customer are inside or outside the European Union.</p><h2 id="cross-border-sales-what-changes-compared-with-italy">Cross-border sales: what changes compared with Italy</h2><p>Selling in several markets (EU and non-EU together) means handling different VAT rates, different sales thresholds per country and, in some cases, customs obligations for non-EU shipments. A common mistake is always applying the Italian rate even where it is not correct to do so.</p><div class="art-nota"><p>The rules on marketplaces and non-EU customs law change fairly often: a periodic check of your position avoids discovering a problem only during an audit.</p></div>

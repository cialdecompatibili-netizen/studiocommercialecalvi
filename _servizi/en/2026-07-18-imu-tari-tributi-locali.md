<p>IMU and TARI are the two local taxes that weigh most on businesses and property owners, and they follow different calendars and calculation rules. Getting either deadline wrong, or applying outdated rates, is one of the most frequent mistakes because each municipality sets its own.</p><h2 id="imu-the-two-fixed-deadlines">IMU: the two fixed deadlines</h2><p>The Municipal Property Tax (IMU) is paid in two instalments: a down payment by <strong>16 June</strong> and the balance by <strong>16 December</strong>. The down payment is 50% of the annual tax, calculated with the rates set by the municipality in the previous year; the December balance makes the adjustment to the final rates for the current year, which municipalities must publish on the Finance Department portal by 28 October. If the municipality does not publish in time, the previous year's rates remain valid.</p><p>IMU applies to second homes, commercial properties, warehouses, shops and building land. A non-luxury main residence remains exempt, unless it falls within the luxury cadastral categories (A/1, A/8, A/9).</p><h2 id="how-it-is-calculated-in-practice">How it is calculated in practice</h2><p>The tax base is obtained by revaluing the cadastral income by 5% and multiplying it by the coefficient of the cadastral category (160 for residential dwellings A/2-A/6). The municipal rate is applied to this base. Example: income 600 €, revalued to 630 €, tax base 100,800 € with coefficient 160, annual tax of about 867 € at a rate of 0.86% — down payment and balance of about 433 € each.</p><h2 id="tari-the-waste-tax">TARI: the waste tax</h2><p>The Waste Tax follows calendars set independently by each municipality (often split into several instalments during the year) and is calculated on the property's surface area and category of use, not on cadastral income like IMU. Municipal resolutions on rates and deadlines must be checked year by year because — unlike IMU — there is no uniform national calendar.</p><h2 id="what-to-do-if-things-change-during-the-year">What to do if things change during the year</h2><p>A change of use, purchase, sale or the start or end of a lease during the year must be declared to the competent municipality by 30 June of the year following the one in which the change occurred. An omitted or late IMU declaration on a significant change exposes you to penalties separate from non-payment of the tax.</p><h2 id="the-risk-of-municipal-fragmentation">The risk of municipal fragmentation</h2><p>With thousands of municipalities setting rates and regulations independently, the most common mistake for those owning property in several municipalities is applying wrong rates or forgetting TARI deadlines not aligned with the IMU calendar. It is a checking exercise that must be redone every year, not a fixed scheme to reapply.</p><h2 id="how-we-manage-imu-and-tari-for-our-clients">How we manage IMU and TARI for our clients</h2><p>We check the updated municipal rates for each property owned, calculate IMU down payment and balance with the correct adjustment, monitor each municipality's specific TARI deadlines and prepare the F24 in good time with the correct tax and cadastral codes — avoiding both late payments and payments calculated on outdated rates.</p>

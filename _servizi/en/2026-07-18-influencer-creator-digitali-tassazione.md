<h2 id="when-you-really-need-a-vat-number">When you really need a VAT number</h2><p>It is not the amount received that triggers the obligation, but habitual activity. A single sponsored video, with no organisation, can fall under occasional services. But if you publish content continuously, work steadily with brands and monetise regularly - even with small sums - the Revenue Agency already sees you as a digital professional, and a VAT number is mandatory. The old reference to 5,000 euros a year concerns only the INPS contribution limit for occasional services, not the threshold for opening a VAT number.</p><h2 id="the-specific-ateco-code-for-influencers">The specific ATECO code for influencers</h2><p>Since 2025 there is an ATECO code dedicated to influencer marketing activity (73.11.03), designed specifically for those who create content and promote products or services online. Using a generic code such as "occasional service" or "other" can cause tax errors and problems with INPS: if the business model also includes producing video or editorial content, code 73.11.02 for the production of advertising content should also be considered. The right code must be chosen case by case based on how the income is actually generated.</p><h2 id="flat-rate-regime-78-profitability-coefficient">Flat-rate regime: 78% profitability coefficient</h2><p>For influencers and content creators the flat-rate regime remains the most common option below 85,000 euros of annual turnover, with a profitability coefficient generally of 78%: tax is paid only on 78% of what is received. The 15% substitute tax is applied to this base, reduced to 5% in the first five years for those who meet the requirements. A concrete example: with 20,000 euros of revenue and the full 15% rate, the substitute tax due is 2,340 euros. Above 85,000 euros you leave the flat-rate regime and ordinary VAT applies.</p><h2 id="inps-contributions-often-underestimated">INPS contributions, often underestimated</h2><p>Many content creators focus only on the substitute tax, forgetting social security contributions. Those working as freelancers are generally enrolled in the INPS Separate Fund, with an ordinary rate of 26.07% on taxable income in 2026 for those without other pension coverage. This cost must always be added to the tax cost to understand the real burden: "I only pay 5% or 15%" is a common mistake that leads to underestimating the overall outlay.</p><h2 id="e-invoicing-is-always-mandatory">E-invoicing is always mandatory</h2><p>Even in the flat-rate regime, the e-invoicing obligation towards other VAT-number holders and towards the Public Administration is now fully in force: a PDF invoice is no longer enough, a system compliant with the Revenue Agency standard is needed. For dealings with foreign platforms (Google AdSense, Twitch and the like) an invoice with a conventional recipient code is often used, with the data reported through the esterometro. Income from platform monetisation programmes is treated for tax purposes like other professional income: self-employment or business income, subject to IRPEF or to the substitute tax in the flat-rate regime.</p><p><em>The information is up to date with 2026 rules and is for general guidance: always check the most suitable regime and ATECO code with an accountant.</em></p>

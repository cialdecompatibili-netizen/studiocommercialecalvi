<h2 id="33-rate-from-2026-what-changes">33% rate from 2026: what changes</h2><p>From 1 January 2026 the substitute tax on capital gains from cryptocurrencies rose from 26% to 33% (2025 Budget Law). The new rate is not retroactive: sales made in 2025 are still taxed at 26%, so the 2026 tax return covering 2025 still uses the old percentage. A gain arises only on a realising event: selling for euros, swapping for a different cryptocurrency, or using tokens to buy goods or services. Simply holding crypto does not generate income tax.</p><h2 id="the-2-000-euro-threshold">The 2,000 euro threshold</h2><p>Net gains (profits minus losses) are taxed only if they exceed 2,000 euros in the year. Below this threshold no substitute tax is due, but the asset monitoring obligation still applies regardless of the amount. Any losses can be carried forward for the next four tax years: it is always worth reporting them even when there are no gains to offset in the current year.</p><h2 id="section-w-monitoring-is-not-taxation">Section W: monitoring is not taxation</h2><p>Since 2023 the tax monitoring of crypto-assets is done in Section W (which replaced the old Section RW). It is a separate obligation from taxation: if you hold crypto on a foreign exchange or in a private wallet (Ledger, MetaMask and the like), you must declare its value in Section W even if you have not earned a single euro. From 2025 Section W is also integrated into Form 730, so employees and pensioners no longer need to file the Modello Redditi PF just for this obligation.</p><h2 id="the-0-2-wealth-tax">The 0.2% wealth tax</h2><p>In addition to tax on gains, since 2023 there is an annual wealth tax of 0.2% on the value of crypto-assets at 31 December, which replaced the old IVAFE. It is due even if no gain was realised during the year: it is a fixed cost tied to ownership alone, to be budgeted together with the substitute tax on gains.</p><h2 id="nfts-taxation-depends-on-your-role">NFTs: taxation depends on your role</h2><p>For NFTs the first question is not how much you earned but what role you play. Those who buy and resell NFTs as investors pay the same 33% substitute tax as other crypto-assets, with a tax cost equal to the purchase price including gas fees. Those who mint and sell NFTs as artists or creators receive consideration that is self-employment or business income, taxed at progressive IRPEF rates rather than the substitute tax: the same logic applies to royalties on later resales. Swapping an NFT for cryptocurrency or for another NFT is in any case a taxable event to be reported.</p><p><em>This area is in constant regulatory flux: always check your specific situation with an accountant before calculating or paying tax on crypto-assets.</em></p>

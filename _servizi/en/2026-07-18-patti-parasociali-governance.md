<p>A company's articles of association are public, rigid and can only be changed by an extraordinary meeting. Shareholder agreements fill this gap: contractual agreements between shareholders — or between shareholders and third parties — that regulate governance, voting and the transfer of shares without going through the Business Register, binding only on those who sign them.</p><h2 id="spa-and-srl-two-different-regimes">Spa and Srl: two different regimes</h2><p>The specific rules of arts. 2341-bis and 2341-ter of the Civil Code apply directly only to joint-stock companies (Spa). For Srl the legislator provides no express rules: shareholder agreements remain permitted as atypical contracts, whose validity rests on the general principles of contract law and recognition of the shareholders' contractual autonomy — with one case of direct application of the Spa rules when an Srl in turn controls a joint-stock company.</p><h2 id="the-most-common-clauses">The most common clauses</h2><ul><li><strong>Voting syndicate</strong> — commitment of the signatory shareholders to vote in a coordinated way at the meeting on certain matters</li><li><strong>Pre-emption clause</strong> — obligation to offer your shares to the other signatory shareholders, on the same terms proposed by a third-party buyer, before being able to sell</li><li><strong>Lock-up</strong> — temporary ban on selling shares (typically 2-5 years), common in startups to keep the founding team together in the early stage</li><li><strong>Tag along and drag along</strong> — the minority's right to be included in a sale by the majority (tag along) or the minority's obligation to join a sale agreed by the majority (drag along)</li><li><strong>Veto right or appointment of a minority director</strong> — protection tools for the non-controlling shareholder on significant strategic decisions</li></ul><h2 id="form-and-duration">Form and duration</h2><p>For Srl that do not use the risk capital market no specific form is required: the agreement can even be a simple private deed, although written form is always strongly recommended for evidentiary reasons. Duration is governed by art. 2341-bis of the Civil Code and varies with the type of clause and the parties' choice.</p><h2 id="why-they-matter-most-when-shareholders-are-not-family">Why they matter most when shareholders are not family</h2><p>In family-owned companies personal trust often replaces (badly) the formalisation of agreements. In companies with shareholders not linked by family ties — joint ventures, entry of investors, equal partners — shareholder agreements become the essential tool to prevent decision-making deadlock and to define in advance what happens in case of serious disagreement.</p><h2 id="how-we-assist-our-clients">How we assist our clients</h2><p>We assist shareholders in defining the governance clauses best suited to the company's real structure — not a standard template, but an agreement built on specific needs — and coordinate the shareholder agreement with the articles of association, with any succession tools (trust, family pact) and with the overall tax planning of the shareholder base.</p>

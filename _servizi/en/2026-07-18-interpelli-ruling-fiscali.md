<h2 id="what-interpelli-and-rulings-are">What interpelli and rulings are</h2><p>An interpello is a tool by which the taxpayer formally asks the Revenue Agency how tax law applies to a concrete, personal case, before acting. The international ruling (now merged into the rules on advance agreements) is the more structured version of this dialogue, reserved mainly for transactions with an international dimension or transfer pricing, where companies agree in advance with the tax authority on the treatment of complex transactions. In both cases the principle is the same: to obtain written certainty before taking on a tax risk, instead of discovering the Agency's interpretation only during an audit.</p><h2 id="when-it-is-worth-filing-an-interpello">When it is worth filing an interpello</h2><p>It makes sense to file an interpello when the law applicable to the specific case is objectively uncertain or ambiguous, when the transaction has a significant economic impact and an interpretation error would be costly, or when there are conflicting precedents on similar cases. It does not make sense for matters already clarified by circulars or previous Agency replies: in those cases it is enough to apply the established interpretation, and an interpello would only add waiting time with no real benefit.</p><h2 id="the-different-types-of-interpello">The different types of interpello</h2><p>There are several categories depending on purpose: the ordinary interpello to clarify how a rule applies to a concrete case, the evidentiary interpello to prove that the conditions required by an incentive rule are met, the anti-abuse interpello to check whether a transaction amounts to abuse of law, and the disapplication interpello to request that anti-avoidance rules not be applied in specific cases. Choosing the right type affects response times and the legal value of the reply obtained.</p><h2 id="binding-effect-of-the-reply-and-timing">Binding effect of the reply and timing</h2><p>The Revenue Agency's reply binds the tax authority only for the concrete, personal case put forward by the taxpayer in the request: if the facts described in the interpello match what is then carried out, the Agency cannot later challenge an interpretation different from the one given. Response times vary by type of interpello, generally between 90 and 120 days; if there is no reply within the deadline, so-called tacit consent arises on the interpretation put forward by the taxpayer.</p><h2 id="what-an-effective-request-needs">What an effective request needs</h2><p>A well-built interpello request must describe the concrete case precisely, without ambiguity that could make the reply inapplicable to the real situation, and must be filed before carrying out the transaction or filing the return it refers to. A generic or late request loses much of its practical usefulness: the certainty an interpello offers is only worth having if it arrives before the action, not after.</p><p><em>Assessing whether and when to file an interpello requires a specific analysis of the case: consult an accountant before starting the request.</em></p>

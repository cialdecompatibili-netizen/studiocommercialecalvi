<p>The annual VAT return summarises all the year's transactions, but before getting there every taxable person must handle periodic settlements — monthly or quarterly — during the preceding twelve months. They are two distinct levels of the same obligation, and must be kept consistent with each other.</p><h2 id="monthly-or-quarterly-settlement-who-can-choose">Monthly or quarterly settlement: who can choose</h2><p>Quarterly settlement is reserved for those who, in the previous calendar year, did not exceed turnover of €400,000 (self-employed and service businesses) or €700,000 (businesses with other activities). Those within these limits can opt for quarterly, with an added 1% interest on the sums paid, which compensates for the deferral of payment compared with monthly.</p><h2 id="the-annual-vat-return">The annual VAT return</h2><p>It must be filed by <strong>30 April</strong> of the year following the reference year. It is the document that summarises the whole tax year and determines the final balance — owed or credit — net of what has already been paid with the periodic settlements. A submission made within 90 days of the deadline is still valid, with the applicable penalties (reduced if ravvedimento operoso is used).</p><h2 id="the-link-with-periodic-settlements">The link with periodic settlements</h2><p>Those who file the annual return by the ordinary deadline of the fourth-quarter communication (end of February) can avoid sending that last communication separately, by completing section VP directly in the return itself — a simplification that, however, requires filing the return in advance at the end of February, well before the ordinary April deadline.</p><h2 id="the-2026-change-on-omitted-returns">The 2026 change on omitted returns</h2><p>The 2026 Budget Law introduced a significant change: in case of an omitted VAT return, the Revenue Agency can now calculate the tax due on its own — cross-checking e-invoices, telematic receipts and periodic communications already in its possession — and proceed directly to collection, without waiting for the taxpayer to regularise spontaneously. The scope for self-recovery for those who forget the return has therefore shrunk considerably compared with the past.</p><h2 id="why-consistency-between-periodic-and-annual-is-decisive">Why consistency between periodic and annual is decisive</h2><p>The previous year's VAT credit, the instalments paid in December, the 1% quarterly interest: these are all figures that must match exactly between the year's periodic settlements and the summary in the annual return. A discrepancy between these two levels is often the first warning sign that triggers an automatic check by the Agency.</p><h2 id="how-we-manage-our-clients-vat-position">How we manage our clients' VAT position</h2><p>We periodically check consistency between VAT registers, settlements paid and data transmitted, prepare the annual return well ahead of the April deadline, and monitor any credit to carry forward or claim as a refund — reducing the risk of automatic checks linked to inconsistencies between the different levels of the VAT obligation.</p>

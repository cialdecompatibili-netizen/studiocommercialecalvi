<h2 id="cosa-significa-davvero-automatizzare-la-fatturazione">What automating invoicing really means</h2><p>Automating the invoicing workflow does not just mean generating an XML file faster: it means linking together all the stages from the business transaction to the invoice stored in compliance with the rules, with no manual intervention in the repetitive steps. The typical workflow includes generating the document, sending it to the SdI, handling the receipts (delivery or rejection), bookkeeping and finally legally compliant storage: automating means removing the manual steps between one stage and the next, not just speeding up the first.</p><h2 id="la-gestione-degli-scarti-come-punto-critico">Handling rejections as a critical point</h2><p>A truly automated workflow does not just send invoices, but actively monitors the SdI receipts and immediately flags rejections, allowing correction within the legal deadlines without anyone having to manually check every single notification. A partial automation that generates and sends invoices but leaves rejection checks to a periodic manual review loses much of the benefit: a rejection not caught in time means an invoice that was never issued for tax purposes.</p><h2 id="regole-di-automazione-per-casistiche-ricorrenti">Automation rules for recurring cases</h2><p>Many businesses have repetitive invoicing patterns: monthly fees, subscriptions, recurring invoices to the same customers on the same terms. Setting up automatic rules for these cases (scheduled generation, automatic sending on the due date, reminders for exceptions) drastically reduces the time spent on routine invoicing, leaving manual intervention only for the genuinely non-standard cases.</p><h2 id="notifiche-e-controllo-non-automazione-cieca">Notifications and control, not blind automation</h2><p>A well-designed workflow does not remove human supervision, it moves it from repetitive steps to the moments that really need a decision: notifications in case of anomalies (out-of-range amounts, customers with incomplete data, missed deadlines), periodic reports on the status of invoices sent and collected. The goal is not "no human oversight" but for human intervention to focus only where it is really needed, not on every single invoice generated.</p><h2 id="integrazione-con-la-conservazione-sostitutiva">Integration with legally compliant storage</h2><p>The last link in the chain, often overlooked in automation projects, is the automatic connection between invoicing and compliant storage: a complete workflow automatically sends every document issued or received to the storage system, without requiring a separate action at year end or close to deadlines. Automating only issuing and leaving storage as a separate manual process still carries the risk of oversights on older documents.</p><p><em>The configuration of an automated workflow must be adapted to the tools already in use and to the real invoicing volume: consult an accountant or an IT consultant before choosing the solution.</em></p>

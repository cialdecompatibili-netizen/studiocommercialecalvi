<p>Holidays, leave and TFR are among the most delicate items for an employer to manage: not only for the payroll calculation, but for the deadlines and penalties tied to incorrect handling. Here is how they work and what the company must check.</p><h2 id="holidays-accrual-and-deadline-for-use">Holidays: accrual and deadline for use</h2><p>Holidays accrue month by month on a pro-rata basis: the annual entitlement set by the collective agreement is divided into twelfths. With 26 days a year, for example, about 2.17 days accrue per month, proportional to the days actually worked (including for part-timers, in proportion to the work percentage). The law (Legislative Decree 213/2004) requires that at least two weeks of holiday be taken within the year of accrual, while the remaining two weeks must be taken within 18 months after the end of the year in which they accrued. If holidays are not used by this deadline they do not lapse automatically, but the employer still risks an administrative penalty from the Labour Inspectorate for failing to ensure they were taken.</p><h2 id="the-contribution-obligation-on-unused-holidays">The contribution obligation on unused holidays</h2><p>A point often underestimated: by 30 June each year the employer must check the holiday entitlement actually taken by the worker in the previous year. If it was not fully used, the company must still pay the social security contributions corresponding to the remaining holidays, with the payment deadline set at 20 August. This contribution obligation exists regardless of whether the holidays are later actually taken or paid out in the future.</p><h2 id="leave-rol-and-ex-holidays-more-flexible-rules">Leave (ROL and ex-holidays): more flexible rules</h2><p>Paid leave, which on payslips typically appears as ROL (working hours reduction) or ex-holidays, follows a different logic from holidays: it covers shorter absences for personal or organisational needs. The applicable national collective agreement (CCNL) sets how many hours are due, how they accrue and by when they must be used — often by 31 December of the year of accrual, but with significant variations from contract to contract. If not used by the contractual deadline, leave can be paid out in the payslip according to the CCNL rules, with generally more flexible handling than holidays.</p><h2 id="what-happens-when-the-employment-relationship-ends">What happens when the employment relationship ends</h2><p>At the end of the relationship — through resignation, dismissal or contract expiry — all unused holidays, ROL and ex-holidays must be paid out in the final payslip, together with the TFR and other end-of-employment entitlements. The calculation starts from the holidays accrued up to the termination date (pro-rating the annual days to the months actually worked), subtracting those already taken: the result is the remaining days to be paid in euros, added to TFR and the accrued thirteenth and fourteenth month pay.</p><h2 id="how-we-can-help">How we can help</h2><p>We help you set up correct monitoring of accrued and taken holidays and leave for each employee, check the 30 June / 20 August contribution deadlines and correctly calculate end-of-employment entitlements, avoiding penalties for holidays not taken or payout errors.</p>

<p>A dispute with a trade union, or with an employee assisted by a union, can arise from a contested dismissal, an alleged anti-union practice or a collective grievance. In many cases there is no need to go to court: out-of-court tools exist precisely to settle the matter quickly and at low cost.</p><h2 id="la-conciliazione-sindacale-cos-e-come-funziona">Union conciliation: what it is and how it works</h2><p>Union conciliation, governed by art. 412-ter of the Code of Civil Procedure and referred to by art. 2113 of the Civil Code, takes place in the so-called "protected venues": the trade unions of workers and employers, where the parties reach an agreement assisted by their respective representatives. An agreement signed in this setting has a concrete advantage for the company: it cannot be challenged by the employee under art. 2113 of the Civil Code, unlike a simple private settlement. This is why union conciliation is the preferred tool when you want to close a dispute — for example a contested dismissal or an exit incentive — with a solid record that genuinely protects the company from later challenges.</p><h2 id="attenzione-alla-sede-non-basta-farla-in-azienda">Mind the venue: it is not enough to do it at the company</h2><p>The most recent case law has clarified an important point: union conciliation cannot be considered validly concluded if held at the company's premises rather than in a genuinely protected venue (the union or the certification committees). The venue requirement is not a formality: it serves to ensure that the employee's will is genuine and not influenced by the employer's presence. An agreement signed in the wrong place risks being challenged later, defeating the whole purpose of the conciliation.</p><h2 id="cosa-verificare-in-caso-di-risoluzione-con-incentivo-all-esodo">What to check in case of termination with an exit incentive</h2><p>Another point from recent case law concerns consensual terminations with an exit incentive signed in a protected venue: signing the agreement does not automatically give the employee the right to NASpI (unemployment benefit) unless the specific conditions laid down by the rules are met. It is an aspect to check carefully before structuring the agreement, to avoid unmet expectations and further disputes down the line.</p><h2 id="la-condotta-antisindacale-cosa-rischia-l-azienda">Anti-union conduct: what the company risks</h2><p>When a trade union believes the company has infringed its union prerogatives (for example by denying information due by law or under the collective agreement), it can act under art. 28 of the Workers' Statute. The most recent case law has nevertheless clarified that not every formal departure from the prescribed procedures automatically amounts to anti-union conduct: what matters is the actual capacity of the conduct to genuinely restrict union prerogatives, not mere procedural irregularity. In many cases this leaves room for an "atypical" union discussion that avoids the dispute, provided it substantially respects the rights to information and participation.</p><h2 id="come-possiamo-aiutarti">How we can help</h2><p>We assist you in handling trade union disputes and grievances, from preparing conciliation agreements in the correct venue to checking the conditions for exit incentives, through to managing relations with the trade unions to prevent claims of anti-union conduct.</p>

<p>Not every activity with a VAT number must enrol in the Business Register, and not every activity that enrols must also file a SCIA. They are two separate obligations, often confused.</p><h2 id="business-register-who-is-required">Business Register: who is required</h2><p>Enrolment in the Business Register kept by the Chamber of Commerce is mandatory for commercial entrepreneurs, companies and artisans. Freelancers enrolled in a professional order are generally not included.</p><h2 id="scia-the-certified-notice-of-start-of-activity">SCIA: the certified notice of start of activity</h2><p>The SCIA is used to notify the Municipality of the start of certain activities, typically retail, food and drink service and some craft activities. It allows you to start the activity from the day of filing, subject to later checks. Note: if you keep operating during any suspension of the SCIA's effectiveness ordered by the Municipality, the penalties for carrying on the activity without authorisation apply.</p><h2 id="deadlines-and-penalties-for-the-business-register">Deadlines and penalties for the Business Register</h2><p>Enrolment applications or REA reports must be filed within 30 days of the date of the deed or event (10 days for deeds of incorporation of limited companies). Late or omitted notification carries an administrative penalty of 103 to 1,032 euros (art. 2630 of the Civil Code): if regularisation takes place within 30 days of the deadline, the penalty is reduced to one third, so between about 34 and 344 euros.</p><h2 id="when-they-overlap-with-opening-a-vat-number">When they overlap with opening a VAT number</h2><p>For many commercial activities, opening the VAT number, Business Register enrolment and the SCIA are handled in a single ComUnica filing, to avoid repeating the same data to several bodies.</p><div class="art-nota"><p>Understanding which obligations concern your specific activity avoids forgetting a mandatory filing or, conversely, filing one that is not needed.</p></div>

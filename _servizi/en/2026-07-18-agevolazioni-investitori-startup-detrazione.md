<p>Anyone considering investing in the capital of an innovative startup in 2026 finds a different tax picture from previous years: the historic 30% deduction is no longer operational, but an even more generous relief in percentage terms remains active, with a narrower scope.</p><h2 id="cosa-e-cambiato-dal-2026">What changed in 2026</h2><p>The ordinary 30% personal/corporate income tax deduction was not renewed from 1 January 2026, because the European state-aid authorisation expired without a timely extension. It remained valid only for investments made by the end of 2025.</p><h2 id="la-detrazione-al-65-de-minimis">The 65% de minimis deduction</h2><p>The 65% deduction remains operational, reserved for individuals, for investments of up to 100,000 euros a year in the capital of innovative startups in their first three years of life. With the maximum investable amount, the theoretical tax saving reaches 65,000 euros, provided the investment is held for at least 3 years.</p><h2 id="i-vincoli-da-conoscere">The constraints to know</h2><p>Investments leading to a stake above 25% of the startup's capital or governance rights do not qualify, nor do those made by someone who is also a supplier of the startup for a significant share of their own turnover. If the deduction exceeds the personal income tax due, the excess can be converted into a tax credit usable on form F24.</p><div class="art-nota"><p>The rules on this topic have moved a lot in recent months: before investing, always check the up-to-date status of the relief and do not rely on information from one or two years ago.</p></div>

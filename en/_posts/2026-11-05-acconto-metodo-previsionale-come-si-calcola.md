<p>The <strong>forecast method</strong> (<em>metodo previsionale</em>) lets you calculate your tax advance (<em>acconto</em>) on the tax you expect to owe for 2026, instead of the tax you paid for 2025. If you earn less this year, or if the rate that applies to you changes in 2026, you can pay much less on 30 November. Here you find the step-by-step formula, two worked examples (ordinary and flat-rate taxpayer), the risks and how to protect yourself from penalties.</p><h2 id="what-it-is">What it is and how it works</h2><p>The acconto is an advance on the current year's tax. You can determine it with the <strong>historical method</strong> (100% of last year's tax) or with the <strong>forecast method</strong> (100% of the tax you estimate you will owe for the current year). The historical method is safe but looks at the past; the forecast method looks at the present, but puts the responsibility for the estimate on you. The choice is yours: use the method that makes you pay less without taking risks.</p><h2 id="when-it-pays-off">When it pays off</h2><ul><li><strong>Revenue or fees down</strong> compared with 2025.</li><li><strong>New deductible expenses or tax credits</strong> that did not exist last year.</li><li><strong>Lower rates:</strong> in 2026 the second IRPEF bracket drops from 35% to 33%, while the historical method still starts from 2025 tax calculated at 35%.</li><li><strong>Activity suspended or closed</strong>, or a switch to the flat-rate regime.</li><li><strong>Higher withholdings or tax credits</strong> than last year.</li></ul><h2 id="how-to-calculate">How to calculate it, step by step</h2><ol><li><strong>Estimate 2026 taxable income:</strong> expected revenue or fees minus deductible costs, social security contributions and other deductions. Use real figures for January-October and a forecast for November and December.</li><li><strong>Apply the 2026 rates:</strong> 23% up to 28,000 euro, 33% from 28,000 to 50,000, 43% above.</li><li><strong>Subtract tax credits and withholdings:</strong> you get the estimated tax.</li><li><strong>That amount is the total yearly acconto</strong> (100%).</li><li><strong>Subtract the first instalment</strong> already paid in June or July: the result is the second instalment due by 30 November.</li></ol><p>If the result is zero or negative you pay nothing and the difference stays as a credit in your tax return. If the total acconto is under 52 euro, nothing is paid.</p><h2 id="example-ordinary">Example 1: freelancer with falling income</h2><p><strong>Simplified example</strong> (gross tax, no tax credits, deductions or allowances): ordinary taxpayer, 2025 taxable income of 60,000 euro, 2026 estimated at 40,000 euro, first instalment paid in June with the historical method.</p><ul><li>2025 tax: 28,000 × 23% + 22,000 × 35% + 10,000 × 43% = 6,440 + 7,700 + 4,300 = <strong>18,440 euro</strong></li><li>First instalment already paid (40% of 18,440): 7,376 euro</li><li>Second instalment, historical: 18,440 - 7,376 = <strong>11,064 euro</strong></li><li>Estimated 2026 tax: 28,000 × 23% + 12,000 × 33% = 6,440 + 3,960 = <strong>10,400 euro</strong></li><li>Second instalment, forecast: 10,400 - 7,376 = <strong>3,024 euro</strong></li></ul><p>Difference: <strong>8,040 euro less</strong> in November. Real figures depend on credits, deductions and allowances: redo the calculation on your own data.</p><h2 id="example-flat-rate">Example 2: flat-rate taxpayer</h2><p>Flat-rate taxpayers follow the same rules on the substitute tax, with a 50/50 split. Suppose a 2025 substitute tax of 4,000 euro and lower 2026 revenue, with an estimated tax of 2,500 euro.</p><ul><li>First instalment already paid (50% of 4,000): 2,000 euro</li><li>Second instalment, historical: 4,000 - 2,000 = <strong>2,000 euro</strong></li><li>Second instalment, forecast: 2,500 - 2,000 = <strong>500 euro</strong></li></ul><p>Difference: <strong>1,500 euro less</strong> to pay on 30 November.</p><h2 id="risk">The risk: a wrong estimate</h2><p>If your return shows 2026 tax higher than your estimate, the acconto was insufficient. The ordinary penalty is <strong>25% of the amount not paid</strong> (since 1 September 2024), calculated only on the missing part. The acconto counts as regular if you paid at least the lower of the historical-method amount and the tax that actually turns out to be due.</p><p>In example 1, if actual 2026 tax were 13,000 euro instead of 10,400, the acconto paid (7,376 + 3,024 = 10,400) would be short by 2,600 euro: ordinary penalty of 25%, that is 650 euro, plus interest. With <em>ravvedimento operoso</em> (voluntary late-payment correction), if you act before any notice, the penalty drops to a few percentage points: for example 2.5% within 30 days of the deadline and about 2.78% within 90 days.</p><h2 id="prudent-estimate">How to make a prudent estimate</h2><ul><li>Start from <strong>real figures</strong> (receipts and costs paid up to October), not last year's.</li><li>Add the November and December invoices you expect to issue and collect: for freelancers and flat-rate taxpayers what counts is what you collect.</li><li>Do not deduct costs that are uncertain or not yet incurred.</li><li>If in doubt, <strong>round up</strong>: a higher acconto is not a loss, because you recover it at the balance.</li><li><strong>Keep your workings</strong>: you need them to show how you estimated.</li></ul><h2 id="f24">How to pay</h2><p>Use the F24 form, Erario section, reference year 2026: code <strong>4034</strong> for IRPEF, <strong>1791</strong> for the flat-rate substitute tax, <strong>2002</strong> for IRES, <strong>3813</strong> for IRAP. Dates, thresholds and the historical method are in the <a href="{{ '/en/blog/tax-returns/second-acconto-30-november-2026/' | relative_url }}">guide to the second acconto</a>.</p><h2 id="tool">Run the numbers with a tool</h2><p>If you are a flat-rate taxpayer or are weighing whether to stay in the regime, an <a href="{% include shop-url.html k="prod" id="calcolo-convenienza-forfettario-excel" %}">Excel sheet to calculate the flat-rate regime's convenience</a> helps you estimate tax and contributions in minutes.</p><h2 id="mistakes">Mistakes to avoid</h2><ul><li>Using the forecast method with an optimistic estimate.</li><li>Applying 2025 rates instead of 2026 rates.</li><li>Forgetting to subtract the first instalment already paid.</li><li>Not keeping the workings of your estimate.</li><li>Using the wrong tax code or reference year in the F24.</li></ul><h2 id="faq">Frequently asked questions</h2><h3>Is the forecast method compulsory if my income falls?</h3><p>No, it is optional. You can always use the safer historical method.</p><h3>What happens if my estimate turns out wrong?</h3><p>A 25% penalty applies to the missing part of the acconto, which can be reduced with ravvedimento operoso if you regularise before any notice.</p><h3>Can flat-rate taxpayers use the forecast method?</h3><p>Yes, with the same rules, calculated on the substitute tax.</p><h3>Which IRPEF rates do I use for the 2026 estimate?</h3><p>23% up to 28,000 euro, 33% from 28,000 to 50,000, 43% above.</p><h3>Can the second acconto be paid in instalments?</h3><p>No, it is paid in a single payment by 30 November.</p><div class="art-nota"><p>Tax rules change often and depend on your personal situation.

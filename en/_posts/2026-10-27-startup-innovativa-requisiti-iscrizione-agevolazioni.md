<p>"Innovative startup" is not just a figure of speech: it is a status defined by law, with precise requirements and a dedicated registration in the Business Register. Having an original idea is not enough to obtain it. But if the company meets the right requirements, the status opens access to measures designed for the first years of life, including incentives for those who decide to invest. In this guide you will find what is needed, how to do it and where to be careful.</p><h2 id="cose">1. What an innovative startup is</h2><p>It is an unlisted limited company, including cooperatives, whose corporate purpose is the development, production and sale of innovative products or services with high technological value. The rules stem from Italian Decree-Law 179/2012 and were updated by Law 193/2024. The status is not automatic: it is obtained by registering the company in a special section of the Business Register.</p><h2 id="requisiti-base">2. The basic requirements</h2><p>All of them must be met together:</p><ul><li>be an unlisted limited company, incorporated no more than five years ago;</li><li>have its registered office in Italy, or in another EU or European Economic Area country with a production site or branch in Italy;</li><li>have an annual value of production below 5 million euro, starting from the second year of activity;</li><li>not distribute, nor have distributed, profits;</li><li>have as its exclusive or main corporate purpose the development and marketing of products or services with high technological value, without carrying out mainly agency or consulting activities;</li><li>not result from a merger, a demerger or the transfer of a business or a business unit;</li><li>fall within the European definition of micro, small or medium-sized enterprise.</li></ul><h2 id="innovazione">3. At least one innovation criterion</h2><p>In addition to the basic requirements, the company must meet at least one of these three:</p><ul><li><strong>Research and development:</strong> R&amp;D expenses are at least 15% of the higher of costs and total value of production. They result from the latest approved financial statements; in the first year, with no financial statements yet, a declaration by the legal representative is enough.</li><li><strong>Qualified staff:</strong> among employees and collaborators, at least one third are PhD holders, PhD students or researchers, or at least two thirds hold a master's degree.</li><li><strong>Intellectual property:</strong> the company owns, has filed or is licensed to use at least one patent, or owns a registered software.</li></ul><h2 id="iscrizione">4. How the company is registered</h2><p>Registration is done online only, with the Business Register of the Chamber of Commerce of the province where the company has its registered office. The legal representative submits a self-certification declaring that the requirements are met. Registration only makes sense if the requirements are really there: these are declarations that must be provable, so it is worth preparing in advance the documentation that supports them (financial statements, R&amp;D expenses, qualifications of collaborators, patents or registered software).</p><h2 id="durata">5. How long the status lasts</h2><p>Following Law 193/2024, the ordinary stay in the special section is three years. You can remain registered for up to five years in total if the company shows that it has grown, by meeting at least one of these conditions:</p><ul><li>R&amp;D expenses risen to at least 25%;</li><li>at least one experimentation contract with a public administration;</li><li>revenue or employment grown by more than 50% between the second and third year;</li><li>an equity reserve above 50,000 euro, built up through convertible financing or a capital increase, together with R&amp;D expenses risen to at least 20%;</li><li>the grant of at least one patent.</li></ul><p>The measures for startups apply from registration and for a maximum of five years from incorporation.</p><h2 id="agevolazioni">6. The incentives</h2><p>The measures concern both the company and those who invest. Among the main ones: tax incentives for investors (personal income tax deduction for individuals, with a rate changed by recent reforms), exceptions to the ordinary company law rules, greater flexibility in employment relationships, easier access to credit through the SME Guarantee Fund, and the possibility of raising capital through equity crowdfunding campaigns. Percentages, thresholds and duration change with the budget laws: before presenting a plan to an investor, always check the conditions in force.</p><h2 id="errori">Mistakes to avoid</h2><ul><li>Thinking that an innovative idea is enough: the legal requirements and the registration are needed.</li><li>Choosing a generic or consulting-type corporate purpose, which rules out the status.</li><li>Registering without being able to document the declared innovation criterion.</li><li>Forgetting that the requirements must be maintained over time, not only on the day of registration.</li><li>Promising shareholders or investors incentives without having checked the current conditions.</li></ul><div class="art-nota"><p>The rules on innovative startups change frequently and depend on the specific case.

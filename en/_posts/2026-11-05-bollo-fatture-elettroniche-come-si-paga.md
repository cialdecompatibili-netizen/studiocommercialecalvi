<p>The stamp duty (<em>imposta di bollo</em>) on electronic invoices is <strong>2 euro</strong> and applies to invoices <strong>without VAT above 77.47 euro</strong>. The third-quarter duty (invoices from July, August and September 2026) is due by <strong>Monday 30 November 2026</strong> with tax code <strong>2523</strong>. It matters especially to flat-rate taxpayers, because their invoices carry no VAT.</p><h2 id="when">When the duty applies</h2><p>Two conditions together:</p><ul><li>the invoice (or part of it) <strong>has no VAT</strong>: exempt, non-taxable, excluded or out-of-scope operations, like those of flat-rate taxpayers;</li><li>the amount without VAT is <strong>above 77.47 euro</strong>. At exactly 77.47 euro no duty is due, at 77.48 it is.</li></ul><p>If the invoice has VAT, at any rate, the duty never applies. On mixed invoices only the part without VAT counts: the duty applies if that part exceeds 77.47 euro.</p><h2 id="cost">How much it costs</h2><p>The amount is fixed: <strong>2 euro per invoice</strong>, whatever the value of the document. A 100 euro invoice and a 10,000 euro invoice pay the same duty.</p><h2 id="on-invoice">How to flag it on the invoice</h2><p>In the XML file you fill in the virtual stamp duty field (Bollo virtuale = SI) with an amount of 2.00. A note such as "stamp duty paid virtually" is usually added too. If the field is missing the invoice stays without duty and you may have to regularise later. Your invoicing software, or a good <a href="{% include shop-url.html k="prod" id="fattura-forfettario-excel" %}">invoice template for flat-rate taxpayers</a>, sets it automatically.</p><h2 id="deadlines">2026 deadlines</h2><ul><li><strong>Q1 (January-March):</strong> 31 May, moved to Monday 1 June 2026 because it fell on a Sunday. Code 2521.</li><li><strong>Q2 (April-June):</strong> 30 September. Code 2522.</li><li><strong>Q3 (July-September):</strong> <strong>30 November 2026</strong>. Code 2523.</li><li><strong>Q4 (October-December):</strong> end of February 2027, i.e. Monday 1 March 2027 because 28 February is a Sunday. Code 2524.</li></ul><h2 id="rule-5000">The 5,000 euro rule</h2><p>You may postpone payment with no penalties or interest in two cases:</p><ul><li>if Q1 duty does not exceed 5,000 euro, you pay it by 30 September together with Q2;</li><li>if Q1 and Q2 duty together do not exceed 5,000 euro, you pay by 30 November together with Q3.</li></ul><p>For anyone with few invoices this is the normal case: many pay everything in November. Note: on the F24 postponed quarters keep their own code (2521 and 2522), not the code of the quarter in which you pay.</p><p><strong>Example:</strong> a flat-rate taxpayer issues 20 invoices above 77.47 euro in Q1, 25 in Q2 and 18 in Q3. The duty is 40 euro (code 2521), 50 euro (2522) and 36 euro (2523). Since 40 + 50 is far below 5,000 euro, everything can be paid on 30 November: 126 euro on three lines of the same F24.</p><h2 id="how-to-pay">How to pay</h2><ol><li>Log into the Revenue Agency's <strong>Fatture e Corrispettivi</strong> portal, stamp duty area.</li><li>Check the quarter's lists: the Agency calculates the amount due on invoices flagged with duty and, in list B, reports invoices above 77.47 euro with no-VAT operations that do not show the duty. For Q3 2026 the deadline to amend list B was 2 November.</li><li>Pay from the portal with a direct debit, or with the F24 (online banking or online F24), Erario section, with the quarter's code and year 2026.</li></ol><h2 id="codes">Tax codes</h2><ul><li><strong>2521, 2522, 2523, 2524:</strong> stamp duty, first, second, third and fourth quarter.</li><li><strong>2525:</strong> penalties.</li><li><strong>2526:</strong> interest.</li></ul><h2 id="late">If you forget or get it wrong</h2><p>You can regularise through ravvedimento operoso: you pay the duty, interest (code 2526) and a reduced penalty (code 2525). The earlier you do it, the less you pay. If you forgot the duty on invoices already issued, speak to your accountant before paying.</p><h2 id="client">Who bears the cost</h2><p>The payment is always the issuer's responsibility. You can agree with the client to charge it as a separate line on the invoice, remembering that this amount also counts in the duty calculation. If it is not in your agreement, it stays your cost.</p><h2 id="mistakes">Mistakes to avoid</h2><ul><li>Forgetting to flag the duty in the XML.</li><li>Applying the threshold to the invoice total instead of the part without VAT.</li><li>Using the wrong quarter code when paying in bulk.</li><li>Paying on 30 November but using the wrong reference year.</li></ul><h2 id="faq">Frequently asked questions</h2><h3>Is the duty due below 77.47 euro?</h3><p>No. Below this threshold, and at exactly 77.47 euro, no duty applies.</p><h3>Do flat-rate taxpayers always pay it?</h3><p>On every invoice above 77.47 euro, because their invoices have no VAT.</p><h3>When is the Q3 2026 duty due?</h3><p>On 30 November 2026, with tax code 2523.</p><h3>Can I pay all quarters together in November?</h3><p>Yes, if Q1 and Q2 duty together do not exceed 5,000 euro. Each quarter keeps its own code.</p><h3>How can I pay without an F24?</h3><p>From the Fatture e Corrispettivi portal by direct debit to your bank account.</p><p>The second tax advance payment is also due on 30 November: our guide to the <a href="{{ '/en/blog/tax-returns/second-acconto-30-november-2026/' | relative_url }}">second acconto</a> shows how to calculate it.</p><div class="art-nota"><p>Tax rules change often and depend on your personal situation.

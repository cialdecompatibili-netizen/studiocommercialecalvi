<p>Opening a VAT number is less complicated than it seems, but the choices you make at the start (type of activity, ATECO code, tax regime) stay with you for years and affect taxes, contributions and obligations. In this guide you will find the steps in order, with the most common mistakes to avoid.</p><h2 id="serve-davvero">1. Do you really need a VAT number?</h2><p>A VAT number is mandatory for anyone carrying out an economic activity on a regular and continuous basis: freelancer, craftsperson, shopkeeper, e-commerce seller, consultant. It is not needed for truly occasional services, which follow different rules. If you are not sure which case applies to you, ask before you start invoicing: the VAT number must be opened within 30 days of starting the activity.</p><h2 id="tipo-di-attivita">2. Choose the type of activity</h2><p>This is the choice that decides which paperwork to file:</p><ul><li><strong>Freelancer</strong> (intellectual activity, even without a professional register): the VAT number is opened directly with the Revenue Agency using form AA9/12.</li><li><strong>Sole proprietorship</strong> (craft or commercial activity, for example a shop or an e-commerce business): you file the Single Communication (ComUnica) with the Business Register, which also forwards the data to the Revenue Agency, INPS and, if needed, INAIL.</li></ul><h2 id="codice-ateco">3. Identify the ATECO code</h2><p>The ATECO code identifies your activity. It must be chosen carefully because, among other things, the profitability coefficient under the flat-rate regime and the reference pension fund depend on it. A wrong or overly generic code is one of the most frequent mistakes and may force later corrections.</p><h2 id="regime-fiscale">4. Decide the tax regime</h2><p>The typical choice for those starting out is between the flat-rate regime and the ordinary regime.</p><p>Under the <strong>flat-rate regime</strong>, income is calculated by applying to revenue a profitability coefficient linked to the ATECO code, and a 15% substitute tax is paid on that income, reduced to 5% for the first five years of a new activity if the requirements are met (for example, not having carried out the same activity in the previous three years). No VAT is charged on invoices. The revenue or fees limit is 85,000 euro a year: if you exceed it slightly, you leave the regime from the following year; if you exceed 100,000 euro, you leave it immediately. There are also other conditions, such as limits on employment income and staff expenses, to be checked case by case.</p><p>Under the <strong>ordinary regime</strong>, personal income tax (IRPEF) is paid in brackets on actual income, with deduction of real costs, and VAT applies. It may be more advantageous if your costs are high compared to revenue, because under the flat-rate regime expenses are not deducted analytically.</p><p>For those starting out, the flat-rate regime is often the starting point, but it is worth comparing the two regimes with real figures before deciding.</p><h2 id="invio-pratica">5. File the paperwork</h2><p>For a <strong>freelancer</strong>, form AA9/12 can be submitted in several ways: online with the software provided by the Revenue Agency, at an office, by registered mail or through an authorised intermediary such as your accountant. The VAT number is normally assigned immediately if you submit it at the counter.</p><p>For a <strong>sole proprietorship</strong>, ComUnica is sent electronically to the Business Register: with a single filing you obtain the VAT number, registration with the Chamber of Commerce and your INPS position.</p><h2 id="inps-inail">6. Take care of contributions right away</h2><p>With the VAT number come social security obligations. Depending on the activity, contributions are paid to the INPS Separate Management (professionals without a pension fund), to the INPS schemes for craftspeople and shopkeepers, or to the fund of your professional register. If you carry out a craft activity, an INAIL position may also be needed. Calculate contributions from the start, because they are a significant item: for craftspeople and shopkeepers, for example, minimum contributions are due regardless of revenue.</p><h2 id="quanto-costa">7. How much it costs to open</h2><p>Opening the VAT number with the Revenue Agency is free. Costs arise if you open a sole proprietorship, because registration in the Business Register involves stamp duty, secretarial fees and the annual Chamber of Commerce fee, with amounts that vary by activity and province. On top of these come certified email and digital signature, which are needed for the filing, and the accountant's fee if you use one. Always ask for a clear quote before starting.</p><h2 id="dopo-apertura">8. What to do right after opening</h2><ul><li><strong>Electronic invoicing:</strong> since 2024 it has been mandatory also for flat-rate taxpayers, with no turnover exceptions.</li><li><strong>Wording on invoices:</strong> under the flat-rate regime you must state the regime wording and the correct nature code, otherwise the invoice may be treated as an ordinary one.</li><li><strong>Revenue stamp:</strong> on invoices without VAT above 77.47 euro a 2 euro stamp duty applies, to be paid virtually.</li><li><strong>Certified email and bank account:</strong> certified email is useful for communicating with the public administration, and keeping business transactions separate makes bookkeeping much easier.</li><li><strong>Document order:</strong> keep invoices, receipts and contracts from the very first month. An <a href="{% include shop-url.html k="prod" id="checklist-apertura-partita-iva-pdf" %}">opening checklist</a> helps you not to forget anything.</li></ul><h2 id="errori-comuni">Mistakes to avoid</h2><ul><li>Choosing the ATECO code at random or by hearsay.</li><li>Entering the flat-rate regime without checking the requirements and the exclusion causes.</li><li>Forgetting INPS contributions and the advance payment deadlines.</li><li>Issuing invoices without the mandatory wording of the regime.</li><li>Delaying the opening beyond 30 days from the start of the activity.</li></ul><div class="art-nota"><p>Tax rules change often and depend on your personal situation.

<p>By <strong>Monday 30 November 2026</strong> you must pay, in a single payment and with no instalments, the second (or only) advance payment (<em>acconto</em>) of 2026 income taxes. It applies to anyone with a VAT number, flat-rate taxpayers included. This guide shows how much you pay, how it is calculated and when the forecast method lets you pay less.</p><h2 id="deadline">Deadline and who must pay</h2><p>The second instalment is due on 30 November 2026 and covers IRPEF, IRES, IRAP, the flat-rate substitute tax, cedolare secca, IVIE and IVAFE. It is paid by anyone who has tax due in their return and no withholding agent holding it back: freelancers, sole proprietors, flat-rate taxpayers and companies. For 2026 the postponement to January granted in the past to those with revenue up to 170,000 euro has not been renewed: the date is the date.</p><h2 id="how-much">How much you pay: the percentages</h2><p>The acconto is an advance on the current year's taxes and is split into two instalments:</p><ul><li><strong>Ordinary taxpayers:</strong> 40% in June (or July), 60% in November.</li><li><strong>ISA taxpayers and flat-rate regime:</strong> 50% in June (or July), 50% in November.</li></ul><p>Two thresholds to remember: if the total acconto is under 52 euro nothing is due, and if it does not exceed 257.52 euro it is paid in a single payment in November.</p><h2 id="historical-method">Historical method: the safe choice</h2><p>With the historical method the acconto equals 100% of the previous year's tax. For IRPEF you start from line RN34 of the Modello Redditi for individuals; for flat-rate taxpayers from the substitute tax due. It is the simplest method and carries no penalty for underpayment, because it relies on a known figure.</p><h2 id="forecast-method">Forecast method: when it pays off in 2026</h2><p>If you expect to owe less tax in 2026 (lower income, new deductible expenses) you can calculate the acconto on your estimated tax. This year there is one more reason to run the numbers: the 2026 Budget Law cut the second IRPEF bracket rate from 35% to 33%. The historical method starts from 2025 tax, calculated at 35%; the forecast method uses 2026 rates (23% up to 28,000 euro, 33% from 28,000 to 50,000, 43% above).</p><p><strong>Simplified example</strong> (gross tax, no tax credits, deductions or allowances), taxable income of 45,000 euro in both years, ordinary taxpayer:</p><ul><li>2025 tax: 28,000 × 23% + 17,000 × 35% = 6,440 + 5,950 = <strong>12,390 euro</strong></li><li>Estimated 2026 tax: 28,000 × 23% + 17,000 × 33% = 6,440 + 5,610 = <strong>12,050 euro</strong></li><li>First instalment already paid (40% of 12,390): 4,956 euro</li><li>Second instalment, historical: 12,390 − 4,956 = <strong>7,434 euro</strong></li><li>Second instalment, forecast: 12,050 − 4,956 = <strong>7,094 euro</strong></li></ul><p>Difference: <strong>340 euro less</strong> in November. Real figures depend on credits and deductions, so redo the calculation on your own data.</p><p><strong>Warning:</strong> if your 2026 tax ends up higher than estimated, the acconto is insufficient and penalties and interest apply, which can be fixed with late-payment regularisation (<em>ravvedimento</em>). The forecast method only makes sense with a prudent, documentable estimate.</p><h2 id="flat-rate">Flat-rate regime: substitute tax and the forecast method</h2><p>Flat-rate taxpayers pay the substitute tax acconto with the same thresholds but a 50/50 split. Example: 2025 substitute tax of 3,000 euro means a 2026 acconto of 3,000 euro, split into 1,500 in June and 1,500 in November. If you expect lower revenue this year and a tax of 2,000 euro, the forecast second instalment is 2,000 − 1,500 = <strong>500 euro</strong>.</p><h2 id="f24">How to pay: F24 and tax codes</h2><p>Payment is made with the F24 form, Erario section, reference year 2026. The codes for the second instalment are:</p><ul><li><strong>4034</strong> for IRPEF (second instalment or single payment)</li><li><strong>1791</strong> for the flat-rate substitute tax</li><li><strong>2002</strong> for IRES</li><li><strong>3813</strong> for IRAP</li></ul><p>The same F24 can include the INPS contribution advances on income above the minimum: check with your accountant which amounts and codes apply to you.</p><h2 id="same-days">What else is due around the same time</h2><ul><li><strong>16 November:</strong> VAT for October and for the third quarter, withholding taxes, INPS and INAIL contributions.</li><li><strong>30 November:</strong> periodic VAT settlement communication (LIPE) for the third quarter, stamp duty on third-quarter electronic invoices, instalments of the tax amnesty plans.</li></ul><h2 id="late">If you pay late</h2><p>A late payment can be regularised through <em>ravvedimento operoso</em>: you pay the tax, interest and a reduced penalty, which is lower the sooner you act. Do not wait: the reduction shrinks as days pass.</p><h2 id="prepare">How to prepare without surprises</h2><ul><li>Take the tax due and what you paid in June or July from your 2026 Modello Redditi.</li><li>Redo your 2026 estimate with this year's real revenue and costs.</li><li>Compare the historical amount with the forecast one and pick the more prudent if in doubt.</li><li>Log the dates in a <a href="{% include shop-url.html k="prod" id="scadenzario-fiscale-excel" %}">tax deadline planner</a> and set aside a share of every payment you receive.</li></ul><h2 id="mistakes">Mistakes to avoid</h2><ul><li>Using the forecast method with an over-optimistic estimate.</li><li>Forgetting to subtract the instalment already paid in June.</li><li>Using the wrong tax code or reference year in the F24.</li><li>Waiting until the last day without the cash ready.</li></ul><h2 id="faq">Frequently asked questions</h2><h3>Can the second acconto be paid in instalments?</h3><p>No. It is paid in a single payment by 30 November. Only the balance and the first acconto can be spread over instalments.</p><h3>How much is the second acconto?</h3><p>60% of the yearly acconto for ordinary taxpayers, 50% for ISA and flat-rate taxpayers, calculated with the historical or forecast method.</p><h3>Do I pay an acconto if I opened my VAT number this year?</h3><p>If you had no tax due in 2025, the historical acconto is zero. The forecast method applies only if your 2026 estimate requires it: ask for confirmation first.</p><h3>Which tax code do flat-rate taxpayers use?</h3><p>Code 1791 for the substitute tax, with reference year 2026.</p><h3>What if I forget to pay?</h3><p>You can regularise with ravvedimento operoso, paying tax, interest and a reduced penalty. The longer you wait, the smaller the reduction.</p><div class="art-nota"><p>Tax rules change often and depend on your personal situation.
